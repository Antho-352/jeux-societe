## What counts as a mystery board game?

**A mystery board game gives you a crime and asks who did it, how and why.** You answer with reasoning, not luck. Five families cover almost every box on the shelf:

- **Case-book games.** You read leads from a book, visit addresses, take notes, then answer a set of questions.
- **App-assisted investigations.** An app plays the narrator: you scan or type what you inspect, and it tells you what you find.
- **Picture hunts.** The case is hidden in a large drawing; you search it for the clues.
- **Escape-room boxes.** Puzzles and codes stand between you and the answer, usually against a clock.
- **Murder games for groups.** A hidden solution, hidden roles or a murderer at the table, for 4 players or more.

The first four are mostly cooperative and work solo. The fifth is where you play against your friends.

## Case-book detective games

**Case-books are the purest detective board games**: no dice, no board to move on, only a newspaper, a directory, a map and your notes.

### Sherlock Holmes Consulting Detective

The original case-book game dates from 1981 and won the Spiel des Jahres in 1985, according to BoardGameGeek. Space Cowboys now publishes it: **The Thames Murders & other cases** holds a map of London, ten case booklets, ten newspapers and a London directory. You choose which leads to follow, read the matching paragraph, and at the end answer questions and see how you measure up against Holmes. The publisher lists 1 to 8 players, ages 14 and up, about 90 minutes per case, and posts a free case on the game's page.

### Detective: A Modern Crime Board Game

Portal Games' Detective (2018, by Ignacy Trzewiczek, Przemysław Rymer and Jakub Łapot) moves the case-book to the present day. **Five cases are played in order and share a hidden plot**, and an online database stands in for your agency's records: police, FBI and military files you search by name or number. BoardGameGeek lists 1 to 5 players, ages 16 and up, and 2 to 3 hours per case; it won the As d'Or expert game of the year in 2019.

<Callout type="tip" title="Try a case-book for free">
Portal Games gives away **Suburbia**, a standalone Detective case of about 90 minutes that you can play without the base game: a young woman found dead in an abandoned house, three days to report. The cards and a mini board are free PDFs on the publisher's page.
</Callout>

### MicroMacro: Crime City, the picture hunt

MicroMacro (2020, by Johannes Sich) swaps the book for **a giant illustrated city map, 75 × 110 cm (29.5 × 43 inches)**. Each of its 16 cases is a few cards asking you to find something on the map; the same people appear at several moments in time, so you trace them street by street. BoardGameGeek lists 1 to 4 players, ages 12 and up, 15 to 45 minutes, and it won the Spiel des Jahres in 2021. The second edition marks cases with symbols so parents can choose which ones young investigators see.

## App-assisted investigations

**Chronicles of Crime (2018, by David Cicurel) puts the narrator in your phone.** Every location, character and item card has a QR code; you scan what you inspect, and the app tells you what you learn for the scenario you picked. The same cards serve many cases, and new stories arrive as app updates. BoardGameGeek lists 1 to 4 players, ages 12 and up, 60 to 90 minutes, played cooperatively.

The trade-off is the screen: the phone becomes the narrator, so agree before you start who scans and who takes notes.

## Escape-room boxes with a mystery

**Escape boxes are mysteries with locks**: you still read a scene and look for clues, but every step ends with a code. In Exit (Kosmos), you can write on, fold and tear the materials, so each box plays once. Unlock! (Space Cowboys) is a deck of numbered cards run by a free app that keeps time and gives hints. Our [Exit vs Unlock comparison](/blog/escape-room-board-games/) covers the two series and eight more.

Some boxes lean hard into detective work. Unlock's free demo **The Elite** sends your investigative team into a Las Vegas hotel casino, to get into the hideout of someone suspected of running illegal games and rigged bets.

## Murder mystery games for adults and groups

**When the group is bigger than four, the mystery moves from the box to the people around the table.**

- **Clue** (1949, by Anthony E. Pratt) is the classic: a character, a weapon and a room are hidden in an envelope, and you narrow them down by making suggestions that others must disprove. BoardGameGeek lists 2 to 6 players, ages 8 and up, 45 minutes.
- **Mysterium** (2015) is cooperative with a twist: one player is a ghost who cannot speak and sends the other players, the mediums, illustrated vision cards to help them find who did it, where and with which weapon. 2 to 7 players, ages 10 and up, about 42 minutes.
- **Deception: Murder in Hong Kong** (2014, by Tobey Ho) hides the murderer among the investigators, while a forensic scientist who knows the answer can only give clues through scene tiles. 4 to 12 players, ages 14 and up, about 20 minutes.

Murder mystery party kits are the theatrical cousin: a script for a dinner where each guest plays a character. They need a host who reads ahead and guests willing to act, and each script is usually played once.

Free deduction games give a group the same tension tonight. In [Dracula's Feast](/card-games/dracula-s-feast/), every guest at Dracula's dinner has a secret identity, and you win by naming them all. **[Night of the Mummy](/card-games/night-of-the-mummy/) is its standalone sequel**, set at a museum masquerade. Both play 4 to 8 in under 20 minutes.

<Callout type="note" title="Something different: Kill Doctor Lucky">
[Kill Doctor Lucky](/board-games/kill-doctor-lucky/), the first Cheapass Game, is not a mystery, because nobody investigates anything: it turns the genre inside out, and everyone is trying to commit the murder. It plays 3 to 7 in about an hour and is free to download, if your group likes the mansion more than the notebook.
</Callout>

## Free mysteries to print tonight

**Every type above has a free equivalent you can print**, so you can test the style before you buy.

<ItemCallout href="/escape-games/the-secret-of-langton-manor-episode-1-legacy/" note="A 12-page printable mystery in an old English mansion, for 1 to 4 players over 1 to 2 hours, with a free app that checks answers and gives hints." />

- **A long solo or duo case:** The Secret of Langton Manor, Episode 1, is the closest free thing to a case-book evening: rooms to explore, cryptic messages and an app that gives graded hints.
- **A quick case:** [The 23:47 Dispatch](/escape-games/the-23-47-dispatch/) is a Victorian mini case for 1 or 2 players: a newspaper clipping, a pawn ticket and a photograph hide a four-figure combination, in 10 to 15 minutes.
- **A mystery for a child and a grown-up:** in The Pumpkin Patch Mystery, the biggest pumpkin in town has vanished before the Halloween fair. One player follows the wagon tracks, the other holds the detective notebook, and neither can solve a lock alone.
- **Against the clock:** Escape Team's free missions are printed puzzles you cut, fold and draw on while an app runs the countdown.
