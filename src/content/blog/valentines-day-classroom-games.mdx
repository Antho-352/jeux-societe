A Valentine's class party usually gets the last 45 minutes of a February afternoon, a pile of cards to hand out and very little prep time. The good news is that **the best Valentine's Day classroom games need nothing but paper**: printouts, scrap hearts and a marker for the board. Below is a ready 45-minute plan, the ten games that fill it, and picks for kindergarten to second grade and for grades 3 to 5.

## What does a 45-minute Valentine's party plan look like?

This plan is built for a class of about 24. It keeps the whole class together for bingo and the challenges, then splits it in two for the hunt and the puzzles, so the room never gets too loud.

| Time | What happens | Games |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Find your partner with a paper heart half | 1. Heart halves |
| 0:05–0:20 | Whole-class bingo, three or four rounds | 2. Valentine's bingo |
| 0:20–0:30 | Three 60-second challenges in pairs | 3–5. Heart toss, card tower, snip a heart |
| 0:30–0:40 | Half the class hunts, half does puzzles; swap at 0:35 | 6. Kindness heart hunt, puzzle station |
| 0:40–0:45 | Closing circle, then hand out cards | 7. Pass the heart |

Games 8 to 10 (Heart Says, the VALENTINE word challenge and Heart draw-and-guess) are spares: use them if bingo ends early, if it rains on the hunt, or for the age group they suit best.

## How do you play Valentine's bingo with a whole class?

**1. Heart halves (opening game).** Before the party, cut paper hearts in two with a zigzag or wavy cut, one heart per pair of children. Hand out the halves at random; each child finds the one half that fits theirs, and that is their partner for the challenges. For extra practice, write an uppercase and lowercase letter on the two halves (K-2) or a multiplication fact and its answer (grades 3-5).

**2. Valentine's bingo.** Our [Valentine's Day bingo cards](/bingo/valentines-day-bingo/) are twenty unique 5 × 5 cards built from 36 Valentine words, with a FREE center square and calling cards to cut out on the last page. The teacher draws a calling card, reads it aloud and holds it up, and children cover the word. Play a line first, then four corners, then a full card for the last round.

Two practical fixes for a class. With more than 20 children, let partners from Heart halves share a card, which also helps weaker readers. The words are all about friendship (best friend, sharing, thank you, kindness), so the cards suit any class.

<Callout type="warning" title="Markers and snacks">
Candy hearts are the classic bingo marker, but the American Academy of Pediatrics lists hard candy as a choking risk for young children, and FARE estimates that about one child in thirteen has a food allergy. Paper hearts or scrap squares work just as well. Leave balloons out of the decorations too: the AAP notes they are a choking risk for children up to age 8.
</Callout>

## Which minute-to-win-it games work with paper hearts?

Minute-to-win-it games are 60-second challenges: one task, one timer, and the whole class counting down. Play them in the pairs from Heart halves, with one child playing and the other counting, then swap.

**3. Heart toss.** Crumple scrap paper into balls and toss them into a box or bin with a heart drawn on it from a line of tape. Most balls in 60 seconds wins. Move the line back for grades 3-5.

**4. Card tower.** Give each pair ten index cards with a heart drawn on each. Fold them, lean them and stack them into the tallest tower that stands for five seconds after the timer stops. No tape allowed.

**5. Snip a heart.** Fold a sheet of paper in half and cut out the biggest heart you can in 60 seconds, then unfold it. For K-2, draw the half-heart on the fold first so children only cut along the line. The hearts become decorations or the cards for the closing circle.

## How do you run a Valentine's scavenger hunt in a classroom?

**6. Kindness heart hunt.** Cut out paper hearts, one per child in the hunting group, and write one letter on each so that together they spell a short message, such as BE KIND or FRIENDS. Hide them around the room before the party: under chairs, on the bookshelf, taped behind the door. Each child finds one heart and brings it back, then the group puts the letters in order to read the message.

To make it a clue hunt for grades 3 to 5, write a clue on each heart that points to the next hiding place ("Look where we keep our library books"). Five or six clues are enough for a 5-minute turn. Our [Christmas scavenger hunt ideas](/blog/christmas-scavenger-hunt-ideas/) show how to write rhyming clues, and the same method works with a February theme.

<Callout type="tip" title="Hide twice as many hearts as you need">
Two groups hunt one after the other, so hide two sets in two colors: red hearts for the first group, pink for the second. Nobody finds the other group's message by accident.
</Callout>

## How do you end the party calmly?

**7. Pass the heart.** Everyone sits in a circle and passes a paper heart while music plays, like hot potato. When the music stops, the child holding the heart says one kind thing about the classmate on their left (or picks a question from a jar: favorite animal, best snack, what makes you laugh). Shy children can pass. It ends the party on a quiet note, just before cards go home.

## Which Valentine's games suit K-2, and which suit grades 3-5?

Younger children need games with **no reading and no waiting**; older ones like a challenge they can win with words or a pencil.

| | K-2 | Grades 3-5 |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Heart halves with letters | Heart halves with math facts |
| Bingo | Teacher reads and shows each card | Children take turns calling |
| Challenges | Heart toss, snip a pre-drawn heart | Card tower, heart toss from further back |
| Puzzles | Easy heart maze, 12-dot heart | Word search, medium Valentine maze |
| Spare games | Heart Says | VALENTINE word challenge, Heart draw-and-guess |

**8. Heart Says (K-2).** Simon Says with a Valentine's name: "Heart says hop like a bunny." Anyone who moves without hearing "Heart says" sits down and becomes a helper. The last child standing leads the next round.

**9. VALENTINE word challenge (grades 3-5).** Write VALENTINE on the board and give three minutes to make as many words as possible from its letters, using each letter no more often than it appears. Vine, lane, tile, alien, native and invent all work. Score one point per word and two for any word nobody else found.

**10. Heart draw-and-guess (grades 3-5).** Two teams take turns sending one player to the board to draw a word from a jar while their team guesses within 60 seconds. Keep the words friendly: puppy, cupcake, teddy bear, mailbox, rainbow, card, friendship bracelet.

## How do you keep a Valentine's party inclusive?

Valentine's Day began as a Christian feast day honoring a martyr named Valentine and is now a cultural, religious and commercial celebration of love, so families in your class will see it differently. **Keep the party about friendship and kindness**, and nobody has to opt out.

- Ask for a card for every classmate or none, and send a class list home so nobody is forgotten.
- Skip romantic games and words. Heart halves is about finding a partner for a game, nothing more.
- Offer a "friendship party" or "kindness day" name if your school prefers it; every game above works without the word Valentine.
- Give children who do not take part in the card exchange a job, such as calling bingo or timing the challenges.
