The class Christmas party is usually planned the week before, by a teacher with report cards to finish and a room parent who has never run one. What both need is not fifty ideas but one plan that fills an hour without chaos. Here it is: a 60-minute timetable built on four stations, twelve classroom Christmas games to slot into it, and a prep checklist you can tick off on your phone.

## What does a 60-minute class party plan look like?

The plan below is built for a class of about 24 children, split into **four groups of six**. Each group spends 12 minutes at each station, then moves on when the timer rings. Stations keep noise down, let shy children join in, and mean every child gets a turn at bingo and at the craft.

| Time | What happens | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Welcome, party rules, split into four groups | Teacher |
| 0:05–0:17 | Rotation 1 | One adult per station |
| 0:17–0:29 | Rotation 2 | Groups move clockwise |
| 0:29–0:41 | Rotation 3 | Groups move clockwise |
| 0:41–0:53 | Rotation 4 | Groups move clockwise |
| 0:53–1:00 | Whole-class game and goodbye | Teacher |

## Which Christmas games work for the whole class?

These games need the whole class together, so use them for the opening five minutes, the closing seven, or as a backup if a station runs short. None needs more than paper and a pencil.

**1. Santa Says.** Simon Says with a Christmas name: the leader gives actions ("Santa says flap like a reindeer"), and anyone who moves without hearing "Santa says" sits down. The last child standing leads the next round.

**2. Freeze Dance.** Play Christmas music; everyone dances and freezes when it stops. Anyone still moving after the music stops becomes a judge for the next round. Unlike musical chairs, nobody needs a chair and nobody is knocked over.

**3. Pin the Star on the Tree.** The birthday-party classic, Pin the tail on the donkey, with a paper star and a tree drawn on the board. Use sticky tack instead of pins, and let each child spin once rather than three times.

**4. Christmas Charades.** Write things children can mime on slips: building a snowman, wrapping a present, a reindeer taking off. Two teams, one minute per mime.

**5. Twenty Questions: what's in Santa's sack?** The teacher thinks of a toy; the class asks yes-or-no questions to guess it in twenty. It has a Christmas pedigree: characters in Dickens's *A Christmas Carol* play a version called "yes and no".

**6. Hangman on the board.** The whole class guesses as one team against the teacher, with Christmas words only. Draw the dashes big enough for the back row to read.

## What should each party station be?

The four stations below are the heart of the plan. Set them up at four table groups before lunch, and **put everything a station needs in one tray** so it can be carried in and out.

**Station 1: Christmas bingo (game 7). Adult needed as caller.** Six children, one card each, and an adult drawing words from a hat. Our [Christmas bingo cards](/bingo/christmas-bingo/) come as twenty unique cards built from 36 Christmas words, with calling cards on the last page. With six players a round takes a few minutes, so each group plays two or three. The bingo PDF is black and white, so children can colour their card while they wait.

**Station 2: puzzle table (games 8 to 10). No adult needed.** Put out a stack of puzzles at two levels and let children choose. For ages 7 to 10, our [Christmas word search](/puzzles/word-search/christmas-word-search/) hides twenty words in a 15 × 15 grid. For younger or less confident readers, the [easy Christmas tree maze](/puzzles/mazes/christmas-tree-maze-easy/) and the [Christmas tree dot to dot](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/christmas-tree-dot-to-dot/) need no reading at all. Finished early? Colour the picture.

<ItemCallout href="/puzzles/word-search/christmas-word-search/" note="Twenty Christmas words, four directions, answer key on page 2." />

**Station 3: a Christmas scavenger hunt (game 11). Adult needed.** Clear one corner of the room and run a short hunt for six, or play Pin the Star on the Tree if space is tight. The [Christmas scavenger hunt clue cards from The Best Ideas for Kids](/scavenger-hunts/christmas-scavenger-hunt-clue-cards-best-ideas-for-kids/) are free for school use; rewrite a few clues to point at places in your room.

**Station 4: paper snowflakes. Adult helpful.** Fold a square of paper into a triangle two or three times, snip shapes along the edges, and unfold. It is a craft rather than a game, but children love the reveal, and the snowflakes decorate the windows until January. Pre-cut the squares for younger classes.

<Callout type="warning" title="Snack and safety">
Ask families about allergies before you set the menu: FARE estimates that about one child in thirteen has a food allergy. For kindergarten and first grade, leave balloons out of the decorations; the American Academy of Pediatrics notes they are a choking risk for children up to age 8.
</Callout>

## Which Christmas games suit kindergarten?

Many kindergartners are still learning to read and find it hard to wait their turn, so **choose games with no reading and no queue**. Santa Says, Freeze Dance and Pin the Star on the Tree are the safest whole-class choices.

At the stations, a few changes help. Swap the word search for the easy tree maze, which has thick walls and a short route drawn for ages 4 to 7. Play bingo with an adult reading each word aloud and holding up the calling card, or make picture bingo with our [blank bingo card template](/bingo/blank-bingo-card-template/) and Christmas stickers. Shorten the rotations to 10 minutes and add a story at the end.

## What quiet games work for the last hour before break?

The last afternoon before winter break is often the hardest hour of the term. If the party is over and there is still time to fill, **quiet paper games** settle the room.

Hand out a maze and a dot to dot to each child, put a pair of pencil games on the board, and let them work in pairs. For older classes (grades 3 to 5), the medium [Christmas maze](/puzzles/mazes/christmas-maze/) is a step up from the easy tree maze.

**12. Dots and boxes.** Two players take turns joining dots, and whoever closes a box claims it and plays again. Draw a grid on the board for a class tournament, or print the sheet from our [dots and boxes rules](/rules/dots-and-boxes/) for pairs.

<ItemCallout href="/puzzles/dot-to-dot/christmas-tree-dot-to-dot/" note="Fifteen numbered dots: count, connect, then decorate the tree." />

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep a spare stack">
Print five extra copies of every puzzle. Someone always tears a page, and a spare stack on the teacher's desk is the quickest way to calm a child who finished early.
</Callout>

## What are good secular winter alternatives?

Some schools ask for a "winter party" or "holiday party" instead of a Christmas one, and some families in your class may not celebrate Christmas. **The same plan works with winter themes**: only the printables and a few game names change.

- Bingo: use the blank bingo template with winter words (mitten, sled, icicle, scarf).
- Puzzle table: our [winter word search](/puzzles/word-search/winter-word-search/), a winter maze and the snowman dot to dot.
- Games: Snowman Says instead of Santa Says, and charades with snow-day mimes.
- Craft: paper snowflakes are already secular.
