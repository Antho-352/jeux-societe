Most printable Christmas games are made for children: big letters, easy mazes, Santa coloring pages. Adults need something else, and what they need depends on the party. A room of colleagues, a crowd of friends in ugly sweaters, your grown-up children home for the holidays and a quiet evening for two all call for different games. Below are twenty printable Christmas games for adults, sorted by setting, with the group size and the prep each one takes. Christmas Day falls on a Friday this year.

One honest note before you start: we have no Christmas crossword and no Christmas word search for adults yet. Where a holiday version would be too easy, we point you to our hard puzzles instead, and we fill the gap with games that need no printer at all.

## What games work at an office Christmas party?

An office party has three rules: **anyone can join in a minute, nobody is put on the spot, and the game ends before people want to leave**. Teams help, because shy colleagues can play without performing.

**1. Christmas bingo.** Our [Christmas bingo cards](/bingo/christmas-bingo/) come as twenty unique cards built from 36 words (Santa, mistletoe, gingerbread, nutcracker, eggnog and more), plus calling cards to cut out and draw from a hat. For adults, try a slower version: no caller, and players mark a square only when someone at the party says the word out loud. It runs in the background all evening.

**2. Christmas trivia quiz.** Split the room into teams and read out the ten questions [further down this page](#can-you-answer-these-10-christmas-trivia-questions). Every answer has been checked against a museum, a library or another source listed at the end.

**3. Two Rooms and a Boom.** A hidden-role game (each player gets a secret identity and a team) by Alan Gerding and Sean McCoy. The group splits between two rooms; the Blue Team protects its President while the Red Team tries to get its Bomber into the same room by the end. Leaders swap hostages between rooms after each timed round. It takes [6 to 30 players](/party-games/two-rooms-and-a-boom/), which makes it one of the few games that can handle a whole department. Tuesday Knight Games offers the print-and-play free on its own site.

**4. Meow!** A card game by Peter C. Hayward based on Mao, the game where nobody tells you the rules. Here every rule is on a card, each player holds a few, and you win the turn you break none of them. [Meow!](/card-games/meow/) plays 3 to 12 in 5 to 10 minutes. Print the gentler Kitten Cabal deck for colleagues.

**5. Christmas light scavenger hunt.** The [Christmas Light Scavenger Hunt](/scavenger-hunts/christmas-light-scavenger-hunt-play-party-plan/) by Britni Vigil of Play Party Plan is a checklist of light displays to spot. As a party game, split guests into teams of four to six, give each team a card and 30 minutes on foot (an hour by car), and ask for a photo of every find. The PDF is free, but the creator emails it when you sign up.

**6. White elephant gift swap.** Also called Yankee swap or Dirty Santa. Each guest brings one wrapped gift, usually of similar value, and draws a number. Player 1 opens a gift. Each player after that either opens a new gift or steals one already opened; whoever is robbed opens or steals in turn. A gift can only be stolen a set number of times, often two, and then it stays put. Agree on a price limit in the invitation.

## What should you play at a friends' Christmas or ugly sweater party?

With friends you can be louder, sit down for longer and play games where people lie to each other. **Hidden-role games** are the highlight here: they need no skill, only nerve.

**7. Christmas Pictionary.** One player draws, their team guesses, and nobody may speak or write letters. **8. Hidden Panda.** A few Bandits are hiding among the players, and everyone else must work out whom to trust with the panda cubs. Each turn you hand out three cubs and place a net on someone you suspect. [Hidden Panda](/party-games/hidden-panda/) is for 5 to 8 players, runs 20 to 30 minutes and needs no moderator.

**9. Dracula's Feast.** Every guest at Dracula's dinner has a secret identity. On your turn you ask a player if they are a certain guest, ask them to dance (you secretly show each other your cards) or accuse everyone at once. [Dracula's Feast](/card-games/dracula-s-feast/) takes 4 to 8 players and 10 to 15 minutes, so you can play three games in a row. [Night of the Mummy](/card-games/night-of-the-mummy/) is the standalone sequel if your group wants more.

**10. Secret Hitler.** Liberals have the majority but do not know who is who; the fascists know each other and try to elect Hitler as chancellor. [Secret Hitler](/party-games/secret-hitler/) plays 5 to 10 in about 45 minutes, and the publisher released the official 13-page print-and-play under a Creative Commons licence. Check that your friends are fine with the theme before you print it.

**11. Most Likely To.** Ask "Who is most likely to re-gift a present?" and everyone points at someone on three. It needs nothing and suits a Christmas crowd: think of who will burn the cookies or open a gift early. Our [Most Likely To rules](/rules/most-likely-to/) describe it as a drinking game for adults 21 and over, and also give a points version with no alcohol.

## Which games suit a family evening with adult children?

When your grown-up children are home, the best games are the ones everyone already half knows. **Card and dice classics** need no printing, explain fast and leave room for talking. For an evening with younger cousins too, our [Christmas games for family](/blog/christmas-games-for-family/) cover every age.

**12. Spoons.** Pass cards round as fast as you can; collect four of a kind and grab a spoon, and everyone else scrambles for the rest. There is always one spoon too few. [Spoons](/rules/spoons/) plays 3 to 13 with one deck.

**13. Hearts.** A trick-avoidance game (each round, the highest card wins the cards played, and you want to win as few hearts as possible). Every heart costs a point and the queen of spades costs 13, unless one player takes them all and "shoots the moon". [Hearts](/rules/hearts/) plays 3 to 6; if there are three of you, see our [card games for 3 people](/blog/card-games-for-3-people/).

**14. Bunco.** A social dice game for up to 12 players at three tables: roll three dice to match the round number and shout "Bunco" when all three match it. [Bunco](/rules/bunco/) is ideal when the whole family comes for dinner and nobody wants to learn a new game. Our [family game night ideas](/blog/family-game-night-ideas/) have more.

## What can a couple play on Christmas night?

After the guests have gone, two-player games with **short rounds** are best: you can stop whenever you like.

**15. Love Letter.** Seiji Kanai's small game of risk and deduction: each turn you draw a card and play one of your two, trying to be the last player in the round or to hold the highest card when the deck runs out. The [Love Letter print and play](/card-games/love-letter-print-and-play/) from Asmodee is free for personal use after signing in with a free Asmodee account. It plays 2 to 4 and takes about 20 minutes.

**16. Cribbage.** Play cards to a count of 31, then score your hand for fifteens, pairs and runs; first to 121 wins. The [cribbage rules](/rules/cribbage/) cover 2 to 4 players, and a [printable cribbage board](/board-games/printable-cribbage-board/) on one Letter page replaces the wooden one. Our [board games for couples](/blog/board-games-for-couples/) have more two-player ideas.

## Which Christmas puzzles for adults can you print for a quiet evening?

Christmas puzzles for adults should be **properly hard**, or they are over before the tea is cool. These four take a while even for keen solvers, and all four have the answer on the last page.

**17. Snowflake maze.** A six-armed snowflake on a 44 × 44 grid, with deep dead ends in every arm and one route through the central hexagon. The solution path in our [snowflake maze](/puzzles/mazes/snowflake-maze/) is 256 cells long. Tip: solve the hexagon first.

**18. Reindeer dot to dot.** A reindeer with tall branching antlers, drawn from 141 dots. The dots sit only on the corners and turns of the picture, so the reindeer stays a surprise until the last lines. Print page 1 of the [reindeer dot to dot](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/reindeer-dot-to-dot-expert/) only, and keep the finished picture on page 2 for checking.

**19. Hard word search for adults.** If you came here for an adult Christmas word search, this is the closest we have: it is not Christmas-themed, but it is a real challenge. Twenty long words (serendipity, quintessential, cacophony) hide in a 20 × 20 grid, in all eight directions, backwards included. Our [hard word search for adults](/puzzles/word-search/hard-word-search-for-adults/) is far tougher than the [Christmas word search](/puzzles/word-search/christmas-word-search/), which is pitched at ages 7 to 10.

**20. Hard and expert sudoku.** The [hard sudoku](/puzzles/sudoku/hard-sudoku/) book has twenty puzzles with about 27 clues each, about 20 to 30 minutes a grid for an experienced solver. The [expert sudoku](/puzzles/sudoku/expert-sudoku/) book has twenty more with only 24 to 26 clues. For the whole holiday, print the [100 hard sudoku puzzles](/puzzles/sudoku/100-hard-sudoku-puzzles/).

## How do you plan the evening?

Pick one game per stage of the party rather than one game for the whole night. Start with something guests can join as they arrive, such as the slow version of bingo or the scavenger hunt. Once everyone has a drink, move to the main event: trivia, the gift swap or a hidden-role game. Finish with a quiet option for the people who stay late, like a card game or a puzzle by the fire.

Print everything the day before and cut the cards on card stock if you want to use them again next year. Our bingo cards and puzzles are black and white, and you can print as many copies as you need at home. If you are also organizing a party at school, our [classroom Christmas party ideas](/blog/classroom-christmas-party-ideas/) have a 60-minute plan, and all our [Christmas printables](/occasions/christmas/) are on one page.
