The best moment in Codenames is the clue that links three words nobody else saw together, and the groan when a teammate touches the assassin. The twelve games below keep that feeling and change the material: fewer words, pictures instead of words, icons, a dial, a pencil, or a room full of liars. Each comes with players, age and time from BoardGameGeek or the publisher. Six have free files you can print tonight, and a quiz at the end matches one to your group.

## What makes Codenames great?

**One clue, several words, and one word you must avoid.** Two teams face 25 word cards. Each team's spymaster knows which words belong to their agents, and gives one word and a number, such as "fruit, 3". Teammates guess one card at a time; a wrong guess ends the turn, and the assassin card loses the game on the spot. BoardGameGeek's description sums up the cast of codenames: "disease", "Germany", "carrot".

**The fun is in the balance.** A clue that is too safe finds one word; a clever one finds three and risks the assassin. A game "like Codenames" needs that same push between clear and clever, and a table that argues happily about what a clue meant.

## Which games use one-word clues like Codenames?

**These three keep the single-word clue and change who gives it and why.**

**Codenames: Duet** (Vlaada Chvátil and Scot Eaton, 2017) is the two-player version, and it is cooperative. You share a 5×5 grid of words; each player sees one side of the key card, with nine agents and three assassins. Give your partner a one-word clue, they guess, then swap. Together you must find all fifteen agents before time runs out. Ages 11 and up, 15 to 30 minutes. It also appears in our [board games for couples](/blog/board-games-for-couples/) and [cooperative board games](/blog/cooperative-board-games/) guides.

**Just One** (Ludovic Roudy and Bruno Sautter, 2018) won the Spiel des Jahres in 2019. One player guesses a mystery word; everyone else secretly writes a one-word clue, and identical clues are removed before the guesser sees them. Be original, but not so original that the guesser misses it. Three to seven players, from age 8, in 20 to 60 minutes; BoardGameGeek voters rate it best with six or seven.

**Decrypto** (Thomas Dagenais-Lespérance, 2018) is the closest for two competing teams. Each team has four secret words. One player draws a three-digit code, such as 4-2-1, and gives three clues that point to those words in that order. Your team must decode it, and the other team listens in: after a few rounds they start to crack your clues. Three to eight players, from age 12.

## Which games use pictures instead of words?

**Dixit** (Jean-Louis Roubira, 2008, Spiel des Jahres 2010) is the picture version of Codenames' balancing act. The storyteller picks one of six dreamlike cards and says a word, a sound or a sentence about it. Everyone else adds a card from their hand that fits, and the table votes for the storyteller's. If everybody or nobody finds it, the storyteller scores nothing, so the best clues are half-obvious. Three to eight players, 30 minutes, ages 8 and up.

<ItemCallout href="/party-games/dixit-print-and-play/" note="Asmodee's free Dixit file: illustrated cards and the rules, for 3 to 8 players from age 8. Print in colour." />

**Mysterium** (Oleksandr Nevskiy and Oleg Sidorenko, 2015) is the cooperative one. One player is a ghost who cannot speak and hands out illustrated vision cards; the others are mediums who must work out a suspect, a place and a weapon from those images. Two to seven players, about 42 minutes, ages 10 and up. If your group also loves solving a crime, see our [mystery board games](/blog/mystery-board-games/).

## Which games limit how you can communicate?

**Codenames gives you one word. These two take words away or bend them.**

**Concept** (Gaëtan Beaujannot and Alain Rivollet, 2013) bans talking. Two clue-givers put markers on a board of icons (liquid, white, food) to make the table guess a word such as "milk", and the first player to call it out scores. Four to twelve players, from age 10, so it handles groups too big for Codenames. Asmodee gives away a demo with the icon board and word cards.

<ItemCallout href="/party-games/concept-print-and-play-demo/" note="The icon board, a selection of word cards and the rules. A free My asmodee account unlocks the download." />

**Wavelength** (Alex Hague, Justin Vickers and Wolfgang Warsch, 2019) gives the clue-giver a spectrum, such as Hot to Cold, and a hidden target on it. They say one word that sits at that spot ("salad", maybe, a little towards cold), and the team turns a dial to guess where. The closer, the more points. Two to twelve players, from age 14.

## Which drawing and building games are like Codenames?

**Telestrations** (2009) swaps clues for sketches. Everyone draws a word, passes, guesses the next drawing, passes again, and the chain gets funnier as it goes. Four to eight players, 30 minutes, ages 12 and up.

**Show & Tile** (Isaac Shalev and Matt Loomis, 2018) is the free one, and the best fit for people who say they cannot draw. You build a secret word with tangram tiles in 90 seconds, then everyone writes a guess for every other artwork. Jellybean Games allows rehosting, so the tiles, 105 word cards and scorepads are on our page.

<ItemCallout href="/party-games/show-tile/" note="12 sets of tangram tiles, 105 word cards and two scorepads. 3 to 6 players, ages 8+, about 30 minutes." />

**Quick on the Draw** is the Pictionary-style team game, and the free sheet gives you four scorecards with five categories each. Bring your own word list and use the timer above.

## Which free party games can you print tonight?

**If your group loves the team talk in Codenames, the arguing over who meant what, hidden-role games deliver it at full volume.** Both of these are free from their publishers.

**Two Rooms and a Boom** (Alan Gerding and Sean McCoy, 2013) splits the group between two rooms. The Blue Team protects its President, the Red Team wants its Bomber in the same room at the end, and leaders trade hostages between timed rounds. It plays 6 to 30 people, from age 8, in 7 to 20 minutes: the answer for a party too big for any box.

<ItemCallout href="/party-games/two-rooms-and-a-boom/" note="Tuesday Knight Games' official free Print-n-Play, linked from our page. 6 to 30 players." />

**Secret Hitler** (Mike Boxleiter, Tommy Maranges and Max Temkin, 2016) has liberals and hidden fascists electing a President and a Chancellor each round. Five to ten players, 45 minutes, ages 13 and up. The game is released under a Creative Commons licence, so the official print-and-play is hosted on our page.

<ItemCallout href="/party-games/secret-hitler/" note="The official 13-page print-and-play and the rules PDF, under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. 5 to 10 players." />

**Three lighter picks for mixed ages:** [Spot It!](/card-games/spot-it-dobble-print-and-play-demo/) (2–8 players, ages 6+) is pure speed, finding the one symbol two cards share; [Meow!](/card-games/meow/) (3–12) is a silly game of secret rules; and [Choose Two](/party-games/choose-two/) is Looney Labs' free two-player expansion for Choose One, a guess-what-I-would-pick game for couples.
