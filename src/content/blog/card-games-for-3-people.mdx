Three is an awkward number at a card table. Most classic card games were built for two players or for two partnerships of two, and a 52-card deck refuses to split three ways. The good news: plenty of games work perfectly well with three once you know the one rule that changes. Here are ten of them, with the exact three-player adjustment, how long a game lasts and where to find the full rules.

## Why do so many card games break with three players?

There are two problems, and every three-player version solves one or both.

The first is arithmetic. **52 divided by 3 is 17, with one card left over.** Games that deal the whole deck have to remove a card, set it aside unseen, or put it in a kitty that someone wins later. Games that deal a fixed hand (five, six or seven cards) do not care, which is why so many shedding and rummy games work at three without any change.

The second is partnerships. [Spades](/rules/spades/), [Euchre](/rules/euchre/) and [Whist](/rules/whist/) are designed for two teams of two, sitting opposite each other. With three, there is no fair way to make teams, so the trick-taking games on this list are played **cut-throat**: every player for themselves. Our Euchre and Whist rules are for exactly four players, so keep them for nights when a fourth player turns up.

## Trick-taking for three: Hearts, cut-throat Spades and Oh Hell

Trick-taking games (each player plays one card and the highest card of the suit led wins them all) are the best fit for three, because nobody needs a partner.

### Hearts

**Remove the 2 of diamonds and deal 17 cards each**; both pagat.com and Bicycle give this rule. Everything else is the normal game: avoid hearts (1 point each) and the queen of spades (13 points), the holder of the 2 of clubs leads, and the lowest score when someone reaches 100 wins. With three, pagat suggests passing three cards left, then right, then holding, and repeating. If you prefer not to remove a card, deal the 52nd card face down as a kitty that goes to whoever wins the first trick.

<ItemCallout href="/rules/hearts/" note="Full rules, shooting the moon, and a free score sheet with room for three players." />

### Cut-throat Spades

Spades is a partnership game for four, but pagat.com describes a three-player version. **Deal 17 cards each and put the last card aside face down.** There are no teams: each player bids the number of tricks they expect to win and scores alone. The holder of the 2 of clubs leads it to the first trick. In pagat's three-player scoring, every trick over your bid reduces your score by 10, so bidding exactly matters more than in the team game.

### Oh Hell (Up and Down the River)

Oh Hell is one of the few classics made for odd numbers: pagat lists 3 to 7 players. You bid the exact number of tricks you will take, and the dealer may not bid a number that lets the total match the tricks available, so someone always fails. With three, the first hand has 10 cards each, then one fewer each deal down to one card, then back up to 10: 19 deals in all. According to Wikipedia, the game was first described in Britain around 1930. We do not have a rules page for it yet; [pagat's Oh Hell rules](https://www.pagat.com/exact/ohhell.html) are the reference.

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep the bids on paper">
Cut-throat Spades and Oh Hell are both bidding games, and with three players there is nobody to remind you what you bid. Write every bid down before the first card is played, then score straight underneath it.
</Callout>

## How does three-handed Cribbage work?

Cribbage is a two-player game at heart, but pagat calls its three-handed form an easy adaptation. **The dealer gives five cards to each player and one card straight into the crib.** Each player then discards one card to the crib, so everyone has four cards in hand and the crib has four, exactly as in the two-player game.

The player to the dealer's left cuts for the starter and plays first. When two players have said "go", the third keeps playing until they cannot stay under 31. In the show, counting starts at the dealer's left and ends with the dealer and the crib. Pagat notes that the two trailing players may team up against the leader for a while, which keeps the race to 121 close.

You need a board with three tracks. Our [printable cribbage board](/board-games/printable-cribbage-board/) is made for two or three players.

## Climbing and shedding games: President and Crazy Eights

### President

President (also called Scum) is a climbing game: each player must beat the previous play with a higher single card or a higher set of the same size, or pass. Pagat says it is best with about 4 to 7, but three works with one change: **with three, there are only three ranks**, President, Vice-President and Scum. All the cards are dealt, so hands are long, 17 or 18 cards each. The Scum still hands their best card to the President at the start of each hand and gets an unwanted card back.

<ItemCallout href="/rules/president/" note="Card ranking with 2s high, sets, passing, the President and Scum swap and house rules." />

### Crazy Eights

Crazy Eights needs no adjustment at all: pagat and our rules deal **five cards each** for three or more players. Match the top discard by suit or rank, play an eight to change the suit, and be first to empty your hand. A round takes a few minutes, which makes it the easiest game here for children.

## Rummy, Golf and Thirty-One for three

These draw-and-discard games deal a fixed hand, so the deck size is never a problem.

**Rummy** deals seven cards each to three or four players (pagat). Draw, meld runs and sets, lay off on any meld on the table, and discard. With three, 30 cards stay in the stock after the deal, so a hand can run for a while. Two-player [Gin Rummy](/rules/gin-rummy/) can also take three at the table: the dealer sits out and deals to the other two, and the loser of each hand deals the next.

**Golf** uses one deck for two to four players. Each player has six face-down cards in two rows of three and swaps them for lower cards over the hole. Kings score zero, 2s minus two, and a pair in a column cancels out. Nine holes on a score sheet make a full game.

<ItemCallout href="/rules/golf/" note="Six-card and four-card Golf, card values, pairs in a column and a nine-hole score sheet." />

**Thirty-One** gives everyone three cards and three lives. Collect cards of one suit worth as close to 31 as possible, knock when you dare, and the lowest hand loses a life. Pagat reports a player's view that the game is perhaps best with three. It is our pick for a 15-minute game before dinner.

**Spoons** is the loud one. Put two spoons in the middle (one fewer than the players), deal four cards each, and pass cards round as fast as you can. Get four of a kind, grab a spoon, and the slowest hand is left empty.

<Callout type="note" title="Still want more?">
Skat, Germany's national card game, is a three-player game with a 32-card deck, and pagat gives a three-player version of 500 with 33 cards. Both are rich but take an evening to learn. For lighter options, [Cheat](/rules/cheat/), [Go Fish](/rules/go-fish/) and [War](/rules/war/) all deal well to three.
</Callout>
