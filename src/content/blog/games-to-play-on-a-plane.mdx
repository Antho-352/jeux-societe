Below are twelve that do fit: five free solo games you can print tonight, two quiet duels for you and your seatmate, two pencil games, and three small boxes worth buying, with what each needs and a packing checklist.

## What makes a good game to play on a plane?

**A plane game has to pass the tray-table test.** We use four questions to sort travel games before a flight:

1. **Footprint.** Does it fit on a tray of roughly a sheet of paper, or in your hand? A tableau of nine cards does; a map that grows in every direction does not.
2. **Loose pieces.** Every token you drop rolls under the seat in front. Fewer is better, and cards beat cubes.
3. **Rolling dice.** A die thrown on a tray ends up in the aisle. Games that roll need a lidded tin to roll in, or a dice app.
4. **Who plays.** Most flights are solo. The rest are you and the person next to you, side by side, not facing each other.

That rules out most of the "travel board games" sold in magnetic boxes. Small card games pass easily, and the best of them are free.

## Which solo games can you print for a flight?

**Five free solo games**, most of them prize-winners at BoardGameGeek's print-and-play contests, where many of the best tiny games come from.

### 1. Mini Rogue

Paolo Di Stefano and Gabriel Gendron's dungeon crawl took first place for best new designers at the 2016 9-Card Nanogame contest. **You go down floor by floor, fighting with dice and managing food, gold and health** to reach a ruby called The Og's Blood. It needs 4 six-sided dice and 8 small tokens, and a game takes 10 to 60 minutes. The file is pay-what-you-want on Gumroad, with zero accepted.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/mini-rogue-print-and-play/" note="9 cards, a scoring sheet and an 8-page rulebook. Solo, ages 10 and up." />

### 2. Under Falling Skies (9-card edition)

Tomáš Uhlíř's original won best overall game at the 2019 9-Card Nanogame contest before Czech Games Edition turned it into a boxed game. Alien ships descend on your underground base; **you roll dice and place them in the base's rooms**, and every high number brings the ships closer. It plays in 20 to 40 minutes. The dice sit on the cards, so bring real ones.

<ItemCallout href="/dice-games/under-falling-skies-9-card-solo-game/" note="The free 9-card original, uploaded by the designer to BoardGameGeek. Solo, 20 to 40 minutes." />

### 3. Orchard

Mark Tuck's Orchard won the same contest in 2018 and the Golden Geek for best print-and-play game. **You overlap cards of fruit trees on matching fruit** to harvest them, in 5 to 10 minutes. It is the shortest game here, and the dice never roll: they count your harvest. The catch is that it uses 15 of them, so pack small ones in a tin.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/orchard-9-card-solitaire/" note="18 cards (9 used per game) and a rules booklet. Solo, ages 8 and up, about 10 minutes." />

### 4. Lawn Party

Michael Erceg's 18-card game placed in the BoardGameGeek 2022 Solitaire Print and Play Contest, 20th overall and 4th for best use of theme. The lawn party is a week away: **nine cards make a 3 × 3 lawn**, every day the grass grows a level, and you mow two straight lines to reveal the snails, flowers and mushrooms underneath, while rocks stop your mower. After seven days, the wildlife must outscore the grass. It needs no dice and no tokens, the grid stays the size of a hand of cards, and a game takes about 15 minutes.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/lawn-party/" note="18 cards in colour or low ink, a 2-page rulebook and an example game. Solo, ages 10 and up; print double-sided." />

### 5. Picklock!

Steven Tondeur's lock-picking game needs **nothing but a standard 52-card deck** and the rules: you probe the four pins of a lock, lift each to its height, and keep the noise down before time runs out. It takes about 10 minutes, and nothing needs printing if you read the rules on your phone. See [Picklock!](/card-games/picklock/).

If you prefer a pencil to cards, our [roll and write games](/roll-and-write/) are single sheets: [Hide nor Hare](/roll-and-write/hide-nor-hare/) is a sudoku-like dice puzzle with a low-ink sheet and three dice.

## What can you play with the person next to you?

**Pick games that stay in your hands.** Side by side, you cannot both read a board the right way up, but you can each hold cards.

### 6. Love Letter

Seiji Kanai's microgame (2012) uses a deck of only sixteen cards, according to BoardGameGeek. **Each player holds one card; on your turn you draw a second and play one**, trying to knock the others out and get your letter to the princess. It is for 2 to 4 players, about 20 minutes, and won four Golden Geek awards in 2013. Asmodee offers a free [Love Letter print and play](/card-games/love-letter-print-and-play/) file; keep score with pencil marks instead of tokens.

### 7. Collapsi

Mark S. Ball's abstract game (2025) uses **16 cards from any deck**, dealt face up in a 4 × 4 grid, and two coins as pawns. You move exactly as far as the card under you shows, and the card you leave collapses, until someone is trapped. Nothing to print but the rules; the grid fits a tray with the cards overlapped. See [Collapsi](/card-games/collapsi/).

For more two-player choices, The Lady and the Tiger packs five games into eighteen cards; see our [print and play games for 2 players](/board-games/2-player/).

<Callout type="tip" title="Use the overlap">
On a tray, deal grids and tableaus with cards overlapping by a third, like a hand of cards laid flat. Most card games only need the number and colour in the corner to stay visible.
</Callout>

## Which pen-and-paper games work on a plane?

**The ones that need a pencil and a scrap of paper**, which you will find on any flight.

### 8. Dots and Boxes

Two players take turns joining dots; **close the fourth side of a box and you claim it and play again**. It fills the back of a boarding pass, and our rules page includes a printable sheet of grids, so you can print a few before you leave.

<ItemCallout href="/rules/dots-and-boxes/" note="Full rules, variants, tips for first games and a printable dots sheet." />

### 9. Sprouts

Draw a few spots: two or three for a quick game, five or six for a real contest. Players take turns joining spots with a line that never crosses another, adding a new spot on each line; a spot can have at most three lines. **Whoever makes the last possible move wins.** See [Sprouts rules](/rules/sprouts/).

[Sim](/rules/sim/) (six dots, don't complete a triangle in your colour) and tic-tac-toe fill the gaps between meals; if your seatmate keeps beating you at the second, read [how to win at tic-tac-toe](/blog/how-to-win-tic-tac-toe/). Our list of [games to play without anything](/blog/games-to-play-without-anything/) adds fifteen games that need no equipment at all.

For children, a printed puzzle travels better than a game: a [transportation word search](/puzzles/word-search/transportation-word-search/) for ages 5 to 8, or a page from our [mazes](/puzzles/mazes/) and [sudoku](/puzzles/sudoku/) collections.

## Which small travel board games are worth buying?

**Three boxes that need no board and suit adults.** Facts are from BoardGameGeek.

### 10. Hive Pocket

John Yianni's two-player strategy game has **no board: the pieces form the playing surface** as you place them. You try to surround your opponent's queen while protecting your own. The Pocket edition holds 26 pieces, including the Mosquito and Ladybug expansions. BoardGameGeek lists 2 players, ages 9 and up, 20 minutes.

### 11. The Mind

Wolfgang Warsch's cooperative card game (2018) is **silent by design**, which suits a quiet cabin. The deck runs from 1 to 100; together you play your cards on one pile in ascending order without talking about them. With two players you try to clear 12 levels. It is for 2 to 4 players, ages 8 and up, about 20 minutes, and won the 2018 Golden Geek for best card game.

### 12. Friday

Friedemann Friese's solo deck-builder (2011) casts you as Friday, helping Robinson Crusoe survive the island and beat two pirate ships. **You fight hazard cards with a deck you improve as you go.** BoardGameGeek lists 1 player, ages 13 and up, 25 minutes.

## Pack your flight game kit

**Cut and bag everything at home**: you will not want to trim cards in a middle seat, and a loose deck is the first thing to slide off a tilted tray.
