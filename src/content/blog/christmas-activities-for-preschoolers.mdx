Search for Christmas activities for preschoolers and most results lead to paid activity packs. You do not need one. A few free printables, a box of crayons and the games every family already knows cover a whole December, at home or in a pre-K classroom. Below are twenty ideas grouped by the skill they practise, with the age each one suits and a 24-day calendar to plan the month.

## What can preschoolers do at Christmas (ages 3 to 5)?

Three- and five-year-olds are very different players, so it helps to know roughly what each age can manage. The American Academy of Pediatrics lists the milestones below; every child gets there at their own pace, so treat them as a guide, not a test.

In practice, that means three-year-olds enjoy **short activities with an adult beside them**, while many five-year-olds manage an easy maze or dot to dot on their own. Ten to fifteen minutes per activity is plenty at both ages.

## How do Christmas dot to dots help with counting?

A dot to dot is counting with a reward: join the numbers in order and a picture appears. Pick the number of dots to match how far the child can count.

**1. Star dot to dot (10 dots).** Our [10-dot star](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/star-dot-to-dot-easy/) has big numbers and wide spacing, the simplest first puzzle. Call it the star for the top of the tree.

**2. Christmas tree dot to dot (15 dots).** For confident counters, usually five-year-olds; four-year-olds manage it with help. Point to the next number if they get stuck.

**3. Snowman dot to dot (40 dots).** Too long for most preschoolers alone, but great as a shared job: the child does 1 to 10, you do the rest, then swap.

**4. Alphabet gingerbread man (A to Z).** The [alphabet dot to dot](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/abc-gingerbread-man-dot-to-dot/) swaps numbers for letters, for pre-K children learning the alphabet song.

**5. Count the baubles.** Every dot to dot comes with the finished picture on page 2. Use it as a colouring page and ask the child to draw five baubles, then three presents under the tree.

## Are Christmas mazes good for preschoolers?

Yes, if the maze is easy. A maze is **pencil practice**: keeping a line between two walls takes the same careful hand control as tracing letters. Choose wide paths and a short route.

**6. Easy Christmas tree maze.** From the star at the top to the trunk, with thick walls and a route that takes a few minutes. It is drawn for ages 4 to 7, and three-year-olds can try it with a finger.

**7. Star maze.** Our [easy star maze](/puzzles/mazes/star-maze/) has short dead ends in each point that are easy to spot and back out of.

**8. Finger first, crayon second.** Ask the child to trace the way with a finger, keeping one hand on the wall, before drawing. It is the right-hand rule from our guide on [how to solve a maze](/blog/how-to-solve-a-maze/), and it means fewer frustrated crossings-out.

**9. Decorate the corridors.** Once the maze is solved, the thick walls leave room to draw baubles and lights inside the empty paths.

## How can preschoolers play Christmas bingo?

Bingo is a matching game: find the square that matches what the caller shows. That suits preschoolers well, as long as **an adult does the reading**.

**10. Christmas bingo, read aloud.** Our [Christmas bingo cards](/bingo/christmas-bingo/) use words such as reindeer and gingerbread. Read each word, hold up the calling card so the child can compare the letters, and let them place a marker. Small groups of three or four work best.

**11. Picture bingo.** For a version with no reading at all, draw a tree, a star, a bell and a present in the squares of a [blank bingo card](/bingo/blank-bingo-card-template/), or use Christmas stickers.

**12. Calling-card memory.** Print the calling-card page twice, cut out the cards and play pairs face down. Children match identical words by their shape while you read each one aloud. Spotting "same" and "different" is one of the skills the AAP lists for three- and four-year-olds.

**13. Colour sort.** Mix red, green, gold and blue baubles (unbreakable ones) or paper circles and sort them into piles. Four-year-olds can usually name the colours as they go.

<Callout type="warning" title="Watch the small parts">
Christmas crafts are full of choking hazards: beads, buttons, small baubles, pom-poms. The American Academy of Pediatrics notes that most children who die from choking are under 5, and that balloons are a risk up to age 8. Keep paper and crayons as the main materials and supervise any craft with small pieces.
</Callout>

## Which Christmas games work in a group?

These need no printing at all, just a few children and some music.

**14. Santa Says.** Simon Says with Santa: "Santa says touch your toes." Give two- and three-part instructions ("Santa says jump, then clap") to stretch listening skills.

**15. Freeze Dance.** Play Christmas songs and freeze when the music stops. No one is out; everyone keeps dancing.

**16. Pin the Star on the Tree.** Draw a tree on paper, cut out a star with sticky tack on the back, and let children try with their eyes closed instead of a blindfold.

**17. Go Fish.** For four- and five-year-olds who know their numbers, [Go Fish](/rules/go-fish/) is the classic first card game, and it plays in 10 to 20 minutes.

## What Christmas activities work at circle time?

Circle time needs activities that **one adult can run for a whole group** sitting on the carpet.

**18. What's in Santa's sack?** Put a familiar object in a cloth bag. Children take turns to feel it without looking and guess what it is.

**19. Story and retell.** Read a Christmas picture book, then ask "what happened first?" and "what happened at the end?". Recalling parts of a story is one of the language milestones the AAP lists for this age.

**20. Paper snowflakes.** An adult folds a square of paper; the child snips small shapes along the edges with child-safe scissors; you unfold it together. Every snowflake is different, and they look lovely on a window.

<Callout type="tip" title="Print a week at a time">
Printing the whole of December at once means lost pages by the 10th. Print one week's activities on Sunday, keep them in a folder, and keep page 2 of each puzzle (the answer) separate.
</Callout>

## How do you plan a 24-day December activity calendar?

An Advent calendar has one door a day; many paper ones start on December 1 and end on Christmas Eve. Use the same idea for activities: **one small thing a day**, 24 days, no paid pack. Tick each day as you go; your progress is saved in this browser.
