A Christmas scavenger hunt turns the wait into part of the present. It can be a trail of rhyming clues around the house on a snowy afternoon, a chain that ends at the big gift on Christmas morning, or a list of light displays to spot from the back seat. Below are all three, with clues you can use as they are, a version for little ones, one for teens and adults, and the free printables we list for each.

## What kinds of Christmas scavenger hunt are there?

Strictly speaking, Wikipedia separates two games. A **scavenger hunt** is a list of items to find, in any order; a **treasure hunt** follows a series of clues to a prize. The party version became a craze in the early 1930s, when the columnist Elsa Maxwell threw scavenger-hunt parties in New York. At Christmas, families use both, and five formats cover almost every house:

| Hunt | Best for | Where | What you need | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indoor clue hunt | Children who read, or with help | Around the house | 8–12 rhyming clues | A small prize or treats |
| Gift hunt | Any age | House and garage | A clue chain to one present | The big gift |
| Christmas lights hunt | Whole family | Car or neighbourhood walk | A checklist and a pen | Most items ticked |
| Elf hunt | Preschoolers | One or two rooms | Picture clues and a toy elf | A treat from "the elf" |
| Puzzle hunt | Teens and adults | Anywhere | Puzzles that hide the next location | A shared prize |

## How do you write rhyming Christmas scavenger hunt clues?

A good clue describes **one hiding place** in two short lines, and the rhyme lands on that place. Five rules keep a hunt moving:

1. **Rhyme the location, not the prize.** "Where the cold milk hides" is a clue; "Santa's sleigh" is not.
2. **Pitch it at the youngest reader.** Older children can help; a clue nobody solves stops the whole hunt.
3. **Use places every house has**: the fridge, the couch, the bathtub, the bookshelf, the shoe rack.
4. **Keep fragile and hot places out**, such as the oven, the tree's lights and anything up a ladder.
5. **Read each clue aloud before you hide it.** If you hesitate, the children will too.

<Callout type="tip" title="Mix easy and tricky">
Alternate one obvious clue with one that needs thinking. Children get a quick win, then a small puzzle, and the hunt never stalls for long.
</Callout>

## How do you set up a Christmas gift scavenger hunt?

The Christmas morning gift hunt is a treasure hunt with one rule: **build it backwards**. Hide the big present first. Then hide the last clue, the one that points to the gift, somewhere else, and keep going until you are holding clue 1, which you hand over in the morning.

- **Write the list first.** Number each hiding place on your phone before you hide anything, so you can fix a missing clue at 7 a.m.
- **Give each child a colour** if several are hunting, or make them solve the same trail together so nobody races ahead.
- **Keep the gift out of sight of the trail.** The Best Ideas for Kids hunt suggests hiding the final surprise wherever most of the toys live, away from the rest of the clues.
- **Treats along the way are optional.** That set's authors put a small treat at each clue, but one prize at the end works too.

The quickest way to do this tonight is the free set from The Best Ideas for Kids: 12 numbered rhyming cards on two pages, with directions. Only the first and last cards matter for the order, so you can remove clues for a toddler or add your own before the final one.

## How does a Christmas lights scavenger hunt work?

A lights hunt is a scavenger hunt in the strict sense: a checklist of things to spot, ticked in any order. Pile into the car, or wrap up for a walk round a neighbourhood known for its displays, and tick inflatables, reindeer, candy canes and whatever else is on your list.

Britni Vigil of Play Party Plan made her free checklist for a family car ride: print one card for the whole car, or one per person to turn it into a race. For a party she splits guests into **teams of four to six**, gives each team a card, a pen and a phone, and allows **30 minutes** to photograph as many items as possible. Her tip: print on white card stock and laminate it to reuse every year.

The tradition behind it is older than the car. According to Wikipedia, the first known electrically lit Christmas tree was wired with 80 bulbs by Edward H. Johnson, an associate of Thomas Edison, at his New York home in December 1882; the first recorded outdoor tree lights followed in San Diego in 1904.

<Callout type="warning" title="Safety in the car">
The driver drives; a passenger keeps the list. On foot, give younger children a flashlight and agree a meeting point before teams set off.
</Callout>

## How do you run an elf scavenger hunt for younger kids?

For preschoolers, the fun is in the story, not the puzzle. The **Elf on the Shelf** is a picture book by Carol Aebersold and her daughter Chanda Bell, published in 2005 and sold with a toy elf; Wikipedia describes it as a family tradition in which the elf watches over children for Santa. An elf hunt simply makes the elf the one who leaves the clues. Any toy will do.

Keep it short and visual:

- **Picture clues instead of words.** Photograph each hiding place (the bathtub, the toy box) and print the photos small, or draw them.
- **Stay in one or two rooms**, so a lost clue never ends in tears.
- **Let the elf sign each card** and leave a small trail of cotton-ball snowballs between stops.
- **End with a treat from the elf**: a sticker sheet, a candy cane, or an easy Christmas maze to solve together.

## What Christmas scavenger hunts work for teens and adults?

Older players want to work for it. Turn each stop into a small puzzle whose answer is the next hiding place:

- **Word search clue.** Circle a hiding place in a printed [Christmas word search](/puzzles/word-search/christmas-word-search/), or tell players the next location is the only word in the list that is not in the grid.
- **Maze clue.** Write a room name at the exit of a [Christmas maze](/puzzles/mazes/christmas-maze/); solve the maze, read the room.
- **Bingo photo hunt.** Hand out [Christmas bingo cards](/bingo/christmas-bingo/) and have teams photograph something in the house for each word; first full row wins.
- **Cipher clue.** Write the location in a simple letter-shift code and put the key on the previous card.
