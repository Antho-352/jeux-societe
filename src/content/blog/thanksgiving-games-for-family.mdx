Below are fifteen of them, grouped by when you play and who is playing, with the player count and what you need for each.

## What games can you play while the turkey cooks?

This is the hardest stretch for children: the house smells of food and dinner is hours away. **Quiet paper games** work best, because the living room is full and the kitchen is off-limits.

**1. Thanksgiving word search.** Twenty words, from turkey to cornucopia, hidden in a 15 × 15 grid. Our [Thanksgiving word search](/puzzles/word-search/thanksgiving-word-search/) is pitched at ages 8 to 12, with words running across, down and diagonally but never backwards, and the answer key on page 2.

**2. Thanksgiving pie maze.** A round maze where the pumpkin pie travels from the oven to the table. Our [Thanksgiving maze](/puzzles/mazes/thanksgiving-maze/) has medium-width paths for ages 8 to 12; younger children can follow one wall with a finger all the way to the table.

**3. Indoor scavenger hunt.** Children search the house for "something green" or "something that sparkles" and tick it off a list. The [indoor scavenger hunt from Play Party Plan](/scavenger-hunts/indoor-scavenger-hunt-play-party-plan/) is free; the creator emails the PDF when you sign up. Keep the kitchen out of bounds.

Thanksgiving morning is the worst time to discover the printer is out of ink. Print one maze, one word search and one bingo card per child on Wednesday evening, and put them with a cup of pencils on the kids' table.

## Which games work at the dinner table with only paper?

At the table, the rule is simple: **nobody gets up, and the game can stop the moment the gravy arrives**. That rules out anything with a board or many pieces.

**4. Thanksgiving bingo.** One card each and one caller who draws words from a bowl. Our [Thanksgiving bingo cards](/bingo/thanksgiving-bingo/) come as twenty unique cards built from 36 words, including turkey, cranberry sauce, wishbone and nap, plus calling cards to cut out. A round takes about five minutes, which fits nicely between the main course and dessert.

**5. Gratitude prompts.** Go round the table and answer one question each. It is the gentlest way to get the "what are you thankful for" moment without putting anyone on the spot. Use the picker below, or edit the list to add your own family questions.

**6. Twenty Questions.** One player thinks of something; the others ask up to twenty yes-or-no questions to guess it. The game is old: the writer Hannah More recorded teaching it at a London dinner party in the 1780s. Limit the answers to things on the table or Thanksgiving foods to keep it quick for young players.

**7. Telephone.** The first player whispers a sentence to their neighbour, who whispers it on, and the last player says it aloud. The joke is how far the message drifts. It needs three players or more, and a long table makes it funnier.

## What games keep the kids' table busy?

The kids' table needs games that two or three children can run themselves, with **no adult refereeing**. Pencil games are ideal: they are quiet, cheap, and nobody cries over a lost piece.

**8. Heads, Bodies and Legs.** One child draws a head at the top of a sheet, folds it over, and passes it on; the next draws a body, the next the legs. Unfold for a ridiculous creature. It is a children's version of the surrealists' "exquisite corpse", itself based on an older parlour game called consequences. This [Heads, Bodies and Legs sheet](/party-games/printable-heads-bodies-and-legs-game/) has the fold lines marked. Try a "turkey edition": every part must be a turkey part.

**9. Dots and boxes.** Two players take turns joining dots; whoever closes a box writes their initial in it and plays again. Our [dots and boxes rules](/rules/dots-and-boxes/) come with a printable sheet of four grids.

**10. Hangman.** One child picks a Thanksgiving word, the others guess letters. Agree on a word list first (pie, pilgrim, harvest) so the youngest players have a chance. Our [hangman rules page](/rules/hangman/) has a sheet with six ready-made games, and tic-tac-toe fills any gap in between.

## What are good Thanksgiving games for adults and Friendsgiving?

Once the plates are cleared, adults and teens want something **fast, loud and easy to join**. These games explain in under a minute, and they double as the best Friendsgiving games because they mix people who have never met.

**11. Spoons.** Players pass cards round the circle as fast as they can; whoever collects four of a kind grabs a spoon, and everyone else scrambles for one. There is always one spoon too few. Our [Spoons rules](/rules/spoons/) cover 3 to 13 players with one deck.

**12. Charades.** Write Thanksgiving words and phrases on slips (carving the turkey, the parade, falling asleep on the couch), split into two teams, and mime without speaking. The charade started out as a written riddle, popular in 18th-century France, and only later became the acted-out version we play today.

**13. Quick on the Draw.** A team drawing game in the Pictionary style: one player draws, their team guesses. This [Quick on the Draw score sheet](/party-games/printable-quick-on-the-draw/) has five categories per card for 4 to 10 players.

**14. 60-second challenges.** One player, one minute, one task: name as many side dishes as you can, stack ten plastic cups and unstack them, or list every guest's middle name. Use the timer below and let the losing team clear the dessert plates.

For a larger Friendsgiving, set up three tables of four and play Bunco, the dice game designed for a party of twelve.

<Callout type="note" title="House rule for mixed ages">
When grandparents, teens and seven-year-olds play together, let the youngest player choose the Charades category and the oldest act first. It sounds trivial, but it gets the whole table invested in the first round.
</Callout>

## Which quiet games work after pie?

Late in the evening, the energy drops and someone always falls asleep. Quiet games let the people still awake keep playing at the table.

**15. Pig.** Roll one die as many times as you like and add up the points, but a 1 wipes out your turn. First to 100 wins. The [rules of Pig](/rules/pig/) fit on a napkin, and the push-your-luck tension keeps even tired players watching.

Then there is the wishbone. Two people pull the dried bone from the turkey and the one left with the bigger piece makes a wish. The custom goes back to the early 17th century, when the bone was called a "merrythought"; the word "wishbone" is only recorded from 1860.
