## How do escape room board games work?

**Every tabletop escape room follows the same loop**: look at a scene, spot something locked, solve a puzzle to get a code, check the code, and receive new material. What changes from one series to the next is how the game checks your answer and what it does to the components.

- **A decoder or answer cards.** In Exit, you dial a three-digit code under the matching symbol on a decoder disk, which points to an answer card that says whether you are right.
- **An app.** In Unlock, you type codes into a free app that also runs the countdown, gives hints and takes minutes off for mistakes.
- **The card itself.** In Deckscape, the solution is printed on the back of the puzzle card.
- **A plastic gadget.** Escape Room: The Game uses a Chrono Decoder: you slide in four keys, and a wrong answer costs a minute.

Help comes in steps. Exit gives three help cards per riddle (a first clue, a second clue, then the solution), and each one used lowers your final rating. Unlock's app gives a hint for any revealed card, sometimes a second hint, and the solution.

## Exit vs Unlock: which is better?

**Neither is better; they suit different tables.** Exit is for groups who like paper puzzles and do not mind a single-use box. Unlock is for groups happy to put a phone in the middle, and who want three adventures per box with nothing destroyed.

### Exit: The Game, the paper puzzle box

Exit launched in Germany in 2016, according to BoardGameGeek. The Abandoned Cabin, one of the first three boxes, won the 2017 Kennerspiel des Jahres. You start with only a book and a decoder disk. Riddle cards appear when you spot their letters in the pictures, and three "strange items" stay in the box until the game says you have found them.

**The defining rule is in the rulebook**: you may write on, fold or tear the materials, and sometimes you must, so you can only play each game once. Scissors are optional but make it easier. At the end, a table turns your time and the number of help cards used into a rating from 1 to 10 stars, so there is no hard time limit.

### Unlock!, the card-and-app box

Unlock is a deck of numbered cards plus a free app. When you see a number or letter on a card, you reveal the matching card. **Blue and red cards combine by adding their numbers**: use an object numbered 11 on one numbered 35, and you look for card 46. Green cards are machines you operate in the app. Mistakes trigger penalty cards that cost time.

Space Cowboys says the app is required, and once downloaded it works offline. Each box holds three 60-minute adventures, rated level 1 to 3. The rules never ask you to mark a card, so a finished box can go to friends. The series won the As d'Or in 2017, and the publisher reports more than five million copies sold.

Space Cowboys gives away four printable Unlock demos and a tutorial. Print one in colour, cut the cards and you will know within half an hour whether the app-and-cards system suits your group.
</Callout>

## Which other escape room series are worth knowing?

Eight more series, each with a different idea of what an escape room in a box should be. Player counts, ages and lengths come from the publisher or BoardGameGeek.

### Escape Room: The Game

The 2016 base box holds four 60-minute rooms in order of difficulty: Prison Break, Virus, Nuclear Countdown and Temple of the Aztec. A Chrono Decoder counts down in the middle of the table. When stuck, you slide a hint card into the Hint Decoder; when you think you have the answer, you slide four keys into the Chrono Decoder. BoardGameGeek lists 3 to 5 players and ages 16 and up. Expansions need the base box, because the decoder is part of every game.

### Deckscape

Deckscape, from the Italian publisher dV Giochi, is an escape room in about 60 cards. The first title, **Test Time** (2017, by Martino Chiacchiera and Silvano Sorrentino), locks you in Doctor Thyme's laboratory. Puzzles sit on one side of a card and solutions on the other, so you need only paper, a pencil and a clock. BoardGameGeek lists 1 to 6 players, ages 12 and up, 30 to 90 minutes. It fits in a coat pocket.

### Escape Tales

Escape Tales from Board&Dice is **an escape room with no clock and a heavy story**. In The Awakening (2018), you play Sam, whose daughter has been in a coma for months, and choices lead to several endings. The publisher lists 1 to 4 players, ages 12 and up and 3 to 5 hours of play, with a mobile app. BoardGameGeek notes a content warning for suicide and suggests ages 16 and up, so check before playing with teenagers.

### Adventure Games

Adventure Games are Kosmos's other series, with no time pressure. You explore locations, talk to characters and combine items, and decisions change the story, which has more than one resolution. Each title has three chapters of about 75 minutes, and **nothing is destroyed**, so you can play again. The first, The Dungeon (2019, by Matthew Dunstan and Phil Walker-Harding), starts in a medieval prison cell: 1 to 4 players, ages 12 and up.

### Mystery House

Mystery House (Cranio Creations, 2019) turns the box into a 3D house. Story cards slot into the box top, and you search the rooms by shining a light through the windows while an app records your progress and checks codes. The base box includes an introduction and two adventures; expansions reuse the same house and app. BoardGameGeek lists 1 to 5 players, ages 14 and up, about an hour per adventure.

### Escape the Room (ThinkFun)

ThinkFun's series is written as an evening for a host and guests. The first box, **Mystery at the Stargazer's Manor** (2015), is set in 1869, when the town astronomer has vanished. BoardGameGeek lists 3 to 8 players, ages 10 and up and about 90 minutes, which makes it one of the few series built for a larger group.

### Escape Room in a Box

Mattel's series began on Kickstarter with **The Werewolf Experiment** (2016): one hour to find clues and crack the codes that unlock the antidote. BoardGameGeek lists 2 to 8 players, ages 13 and up, and describes it as a one-time play. The later Flashback can be played up to three times along different paths, according to the publisher's description quoted there.

### Escape Team

Escape Team is not a box at all: **you print each mission as a PDF** and play with a free app that narrates in audio, runs the countdown and checks five-digit codes. Puzzles are cut, folded and drawn on, like Exit, but you can simply print a fresh copy. The site lists missions for 2 to 4 players, from a 10-minute training mission to 40-minute missions; the training mission and Mission 1 are free.

## Which escape room game should you start with?

**Start with the free demos, then buy for the people at your table**, not for the most famous name.

- **Exit:** an easy box. Thames & Kosmos rates **The Sunken Treasure** 2 of 5 for ages 10 and up and sells it in a beginner bundle with The Haunted Roller Coaster, The Mysterious Museum and The House of Riddles.
- **Unlock:** **Escape Adventures**, the first box, which BoardGameGeek notes includes a ten-card tutorial. Or play the free tutorial and demos first.
- **Young children:** **EXIT Kids: Jungle of Riddles** is for ages 5 to 7, takes 20 minutes and recombines its puzzle cards so it can be replayed. EXIT Family boxes hold two adventures for ages 8 and up.
- **Two players or solo:** Exit, Unlock, Deckscape, Escape Tales and Adventure Games all start at one player. Free options built for two include [The Last Trick-or-Treat](/escape-games/the-last-trick-or-treat/) and [Lost in the Woods](/escape-games/lost-in-the-woods-minor-arcana-games/).
- **A party of six or more:** Escape the Room (up to 8) or Escape Room in a Box (up to 8), or one of our free DIY kits you hide around the house.
- **Story lovers who hate timers:** Adventure Games for a lighter tone, Escape Tales for a darker one.

<Callout type="note" title="Difficulty is relative">
Each publisher uses its own scale: Exit rates boxes 1 to 5, Unlock rates each adventure 1 to 3, and Escape Room: The Game orders its rooms from easiest to hardest. A level 2 in one series is not a level 2 in another, so start low in any series you have not played.
</Callout>
