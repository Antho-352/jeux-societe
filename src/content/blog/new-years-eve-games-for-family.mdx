Staying up until midnight is the whole point of New Year's Eve for children, and the hardest part for parents. The trick is to fill the evening with short games that get louder as the night goes on, and to give the youngest a midnight of their own. Below is an hour-by-hour plan with twelve games, sorted by age, almost all of them free to print or played with a deck of cards.

## An hour-by-hour New Year's Eve plan

**One game per hour, quiet games first and noisy ones last.** A puzzle after dinner settles everyone; a dice game with shouting keeps them awake at 11 pm. Swap any slot for another game from the list below.

| Time | What to play | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|
| 6 pm | [New Year's Eve bingo](/bingo/blank-bingo-card-template/), filled in over dinner | Everyone |
| 7 pm | Fake midnight for little ones (London's New Year, US Eastern time) | Under 7s |
| 7:30 pm | [Spoons](/rules/spoons/) or [Snakes and Ladders](/rules/snakes-and-ladders/) | Ages 4 and up |
| 8 pm | Word search race and maze relay | Ages 7 to adults |
| 9 pm | An escape game demo or Hidden Panda | Tweens and teens |
| 10 pm | Drawing games: Heads, Bodies and Legs, Quick on the Draw | Everyone |
| 11 pm | Year-in-review prompts and resolution guessing | Everyone |
| 11:30 pm | [Left Right Center](/rules/left-right-center/) until the countdown | The whole room |

West Coast families can also use New York's midnight as a second, later "fake" countdown: the Times Square ball reaches midnight at 9 pm Pacific.

## Games for little ones (with a fake midnight)

**A fake midnight is a full countdown at an earlier hour**, and it works best when it is a real New Year somewhere. The UK is on Greenwich Mean Time in winter, five hours ahead of US Eastern time, so London's midnight falls at 7 pm in New York and 4 pm in Los Angeles.

| Midnight in | US Eastern | US Central | US Pacific | UK |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kiritimati, Kiribati (earliest time zone) | 5 am | 4 am | 2 am | 10 am |
| Dubai | 3 pm | 2 pm | noon | 8 pm |
| Paris, Madrid, Berlin | 6 pm | 5 pm | 3 pm | 11 pm |
| London | 7 pm | 6 pm | 4 pm | Midnight |
| New York | Midnight | 11 pm | 9 pm | 5 am (Jan 1) |

All times are on December 31 unless marked. Kiritimati is on UTC+14, the earliest time zone on Earth.

Before the countdown, keep games short and luck-based so nobody cries over a loss. **Spoons** needs a deck and one spoon fewer than the players; our rules page puts it at ages 4 and up. **Snakes and Ladders** is pure luck, which makes it fair for a preschooler against a grandparent, and our rules page has a free printable board.

For the very youngest, make **mini countdown bingo** on the [blank 3×3 bingo card](/bingo/blank-3x3-bingo-card-template/): draw nine things they will see tonight (a clock, a balloon, a party hat, a star) and let them colour each one when it appears.

<Callout type="tip" title="Make the fake midnight count">
Set a phone alarm, dim the lights, count down from ten together and blow noisemakers. In Spain people mark the twelve strokes of midnight by eating a grape on each one; little ones can do twelve jumps or twelve claps instead.
</Callout>

## Games for tweens and teens

Older children stay up gladly if the games have a bit of tension.

**Run a maze relay.** Print the [winter maze](/puzzles/mazes/winter-maze/) (24 × 24, ages 8 to 12) for the younger team and the 44-cell snowflake maze for the adults, start the timer, and see which team finishes first. **A word search race** works the same way: everyone gets the same winter word search and the first to circle all twenty words wins.

<ItemCallout href="/puzzles/mazes/snowflake-maze/" note="A six-armed snowflake maze, 44 cells wide, with deep dead ends. Hard enough for adults; solution on page 2." />

<Timer seconds={120} title="Maze relay and word search timer" />

**Try an escape game at 9 pm.** The free Unlock! demos from Space Cowboys take about 30 minutes for 1 to 6 players from age 10: print and cut the cards, then play with the free app. [Unlock! 5th Avenue](/escape-games/unlock-demo-5th-avenue/) is set in New York in 1931, a good warm-up for the ball drop.

**Hidden Panda** is a 20-minute social deduction game for 5 to 8 players from age 8: hand baby pandas to the players you trust and net the ones you suspect, without giving away whether you are a Panda or a Bandit. For a bigger group, [Meow!](/card-games/meow/) seats up to 12.

## Whole-family games with paper, pencils and dice

**New Year's Eve bingo is the game that runs all evening.** Print the blank 5×5 card and fill the 24 squares with things that will happen tonight: someone yawns, someone says "Happy New Year" early, the dog barks at fireworks, a toast, confetti on the floor, a song on TV. Mark a square when it happens; the first full line picks the next game.

<ItemCallout href="/bingo/blank-bingo-card-template/" note="A blank 5×5 card with a FREE centre square: one large card per page, plus a page with two smaller ones." />

**Drawing games** level the field between ages. In **Heads, Bodies and Legs**, one player draws a head, folds the paper over and passes it on for a body, then legs; nobody sees the creature until the end. Make it a theme: everyone draws next year's mascot. **Quick on the Draw** is a team drawing game in the style of Pictionary; our printable gives the category cards, and you write the word list from your family's year.

<Timer seconds={60} title="One-minute drawing timer" />

**In the last half hour, play a dice game that fits the whole room.** [Left Right Center](/rules/left-right-center/) needs three dice and three chips or coins per player: roll, pass chips left, right or into the centre, and the last player holding chips wins the pot. It takes about 20 minutes, so start it around 11:30 pm and finish on the countdown. For a longer game across several tables, [Bunco](/rules/bunco/) seats up to 12.

## Year-in-review and resolution games

**The best game of the evening is often the one about your own year.** Spin the picker for a prompt, go round the table, and keep the answers short. Edit the list to suit your family.

Then play **resolution guessing**. Everyone writes one resolution on a slip of paper, folds it and drops it in a bowl. One person reads them aloud, and the others guess who wrote each. Keep the slips in an envelope marked with next year's date and read them again next New Year's Eve.
