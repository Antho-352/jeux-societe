A bingo pattern is the shape a player has to cover before shouting "Bingo!": a line, the four corners, a letter, a picture frame or the whole card. Changing the pattern is the easiest way to make a bingo afternoon last, because it changes how long each game takes. Below are 30 bingo patterns, each drawn on a 5×5 card, with the number of calls each one needs, measured on our own 20 printable cards. Blackout bingo, the longest, is explained in its own section.

## What is a bingo pattern?

In standard American bingo, each card has 25 squares in five columns headed B, I, N, G and O, with the numbers 1 to 75 split between them (B holds 1 to 15, O holds 61 to 75). The middle square is a FREE space, already covered at the start. The caller draws numbers at random, players cover them, and the first player to complete the agreed pattern wins. Wikipedia's article describes the classic win as a horizontal, vertical or diagonal line, and lists the four corners, the postage stamp, the roving L and the blackout among the special games.

Two things decide how long a pattern takes:

- **How many squares it needs.** A line needs 4 numbers plus the FREE space; blackout needs all 24.
- **How many ways it can be made.** "Any line" can be won 12 ways (5 rows, 5 columns, 2 diagonals); the letter X only one way. Bingo suppliers print this as "ways to win" in their pattern guides.

## How many calls does each pattern take?

**The number of calls is the length of the game.** To measure it, we took the 20 cards in our [printable 1–75 bingo cards](/bingo/bingo-cards-1-75/), exactly as they are printed, and had a small Python program play 100,000 games: shuffle the 75 numbers, call them in that order, and note the call on which the first card completes each pattern. The FREE square counts as covered, except for the hard-way line.

Fewer cards make longer games, and a hall with hundreds of cards is much faster. Arrow International, a bingo supplier, publishes the same kind of figures from 70,000 simulated games for halls: with 500 cards, its guide gives about 12 calls for any straight line and about 60 for a coverall. With 20 cards at home, expect roughly twice as many calls for a line and 67 for a blackout.

## Line patterns: horizontal, vertical, diagonal and four corners

<Figure src="/img/blog/bingo-patterns/line-patterns.svg" alt="Eight bingo cards showing any line, horizontal line, vertical line, diagonal line, hard-way line, four corners, line or four corners and two lines" caption="Line patterns. The FREE square counts in every line through the center, except in a hard-way line." width={1060} height={600} />

**Any line** is the classic game and the one to start with: five in a row across, down or diagonally. It has 12 ways to win, so it ends in about 22 calls with 20 cards. Calling **only horizontal**, **only vertical** or **only diagonal** lines cuts the ways to win and adds 5 to 7 calls.

Does the direction matter? A 2017 *Math Horizons* paper by Arthur Benjamin, Joseph Kisenwether and Ben Weiss, cited by Wikipedia, shows that horizontal lines become about three times more likely than vertical ones when very many cards are in play. In a home game you will not notice it: in our 20-card simulation, 35% of the winning cards had a horizontal line, 34% a vertical one and 31% a diagonal one.

**Hard-way line** is a line that must be made without the FREE square, so it uses one of the 8 lines that avoid the center and needs 5 numbers. **Four corners** needs only 4 numbers, but they must be those exact 4, which is why it takes longer than a line (about 35 calls). **Line or four corners** accepts either. **Two lines** asks for any two lines at once and fits nicely between a quick game and a long one.

## Blackout bingo (coverall): rules and how long it takes

**Blackout bingo, also called coverall, is won by covering every square on the card**: all 24 numbers plus the FREE space. It is the longest game, so halls usually keep it for the end of a session; Wikipedia notes that the final game is often a coverall with a larger prize, sometimes a jackpot that must be won within a set number of calls.

With our 20 cards, the first blackout came after **67 calls on average**, and between 63 and 70 calls in 80% of games. A single card on its own needs about 73 of the 75 numbers. If you call one number every 10 seconds, a blackout round lasts about 11 minutes; any line, about 4.

Blackout also produces the most shared wins: in 18% of our games, two or more cards completed on the same call. Agree beforehand on what happens then (share the prize, or play one more number between the tied players). Some halls add a **second-chance coverall**: Arrow's guide describes playing on after the first winner until another card is covered.

## Small shapes: postage stamp, blocks and kites

<Figure src="/img/blog/bingo-patterns/small-shapes.svg" alt="Six bingo cards showing postage stamp, block of 4, six pack, block of 9, small frame and crazy kite" caption="Small shapes. Each card shows one way to win; most of these patterns may sit anywhere the shape fits." width={1060} height={600} />

**Postage stamp** is a 2×2 block in a corner. Wikipedia describes it in the upper right; we counted any of the four corners, as Arrow's "any corner stamp" does. It ends in about 25 calls. **Block of 4** is the same 2×2 shape anywhere on the card, 16 ways, and the fastest pattern on this page at about 16 calls: good for a warm-up round.

**Six pack** (any 2×3 block) and **block of 9** (any 3×3 square) are bigger blocks; the block of 9 takes about 40 calls. **Small frame** is the ring of 8 squares around the FREE space and is a long game, about 51 calls, because it can only be made one way. **Crazy kite** is a diagonal from one corner to the center with a 2×2 block in the opposite corner, allowed in any of the four corners.

## Shape patterns: letters X, T and L, and picture frame

<Figure src="/img/blog/bingo-patterns/letter-patterns.svg" alt="Eight bingo cards showing the letters X, T and L, a crazy L, a plus sign, the letters H and Z, and the number 7" caption="Letters and numbers. Only the crazy L may be turned; the others have one fixed position." width={1060} height={600} />

Letters are the patterns players remember. **Letter X** covers both diagonals (8 numbers plus FREE), **letter T** the top row and the N column, **letter L** the B column and the bottom row. All three take around 50 calls with 20 cards. A **crazy** pattern can face any way: Wikipedia's "roving L" (all the B's or all the O's, plus the top or bottom row) is a crazy L, and allowing the four positions brings the game down from 53 to 47 calls.

The **plus sign** (middle row and N column) and the **number 7** play like the X. **Letter H** and **letter Z** are bigger, 12 numbers each, and run to nearly 60 calls.

<Figure src="/img/blog/bingo-patterns/picture-patterns.svg" alt="Eight bingo cards showing picture frame, diamond, Christmas tree, bow tie, layer cake, tic-tac-toe, checkerboard and blackout" caption="Picture patterns, from the diamond (12 numbers) to blackout (24)." width={1060} height={600} />

**Picture frame** (also called large frame) covers the 16 squares around the edge of the card and is the classic long game before blackout, about 62 calls. **Bow tie** and **tic-tac-toe** need 16 numbers too and take just as long. **Layer cake** (rows 1, 3 and 5) and the **checkerboard** are good for a change of rhythm; the **diamond** and the **Christmas tree** suit seasonal parties, and our [Christmas bingo cards](/bingo/christmas-bingo/) work with any of these patterns.

## Easy patterns for kids, classrooms and seniors

**For young children, short games matter more than clever shapes.** Pick patterns that end within about 30 calls and are easy to see: block of 4, any line, postage stamp, four corners. With a class of 20 to 25, play any line first, then four corners, and finish with a letter that matches the lesson (T for Thanksgiving, X for a kiss on Valentine's Day). Our [classroom bingo cards](/bingo/classroom-bingo/) use words instead of numbers, and our [Valentine's Day classroom games](/blog/valentines-day-classroom-games/) and [classroom Christmas party ideas](/blog/classroom-christmas-party-ideas/) build bingo into a full party.

**For a care home or a group of older players, choose familiar shapes and a steady rhythm.** Any line, four corners, the letters X and T and the picture frame are easy to picture without checking the board. Call each number twice, clearly, and keep blackout for the last game. Print the cards on card stock so they do not slide, and use counters or dry beans rather than daubers if hands are less steady.

**For a long family evening**, follow the classic hall order: a quick line, two lines, a letter, the picture frame, then blackout. The table above lets you plan the length of each round.

## How to announce and check a pattern

1. **Draw the pattern before the first call.** Sketch it on a whiteboard or hold up a card with the squares colored in. Our [blank bingo card template](/bingo/blank-bingo-card-template/) is ideal for this: color the pattern on a blank card and prop it up for everyone to see.
2. **Say whether the FREE square counts** and whether the pattern is fixed or "crazy" (any orientation).
3. **Cross off every call** on a call sheet so you can check a claim. In a hall, the caller or an assistant examines every claimed card before the win counts, as Wikipedia describes.
4. **Read back the winning numbers** from the card and check each one against the sheet. A false claim is common and harmless; carry on calling.
5. **Settle ties in advance.** Two or more players finished on the same call in 7 to 18% of our games, depending on the pattern.

## Bingo variations beyond patterns

Patterns are not the only way to change the game. Wikipedia lists several types of bingo games played in halls, and most work at home:

- **Wild numbers.** The first number called makes others wild: if it is 22, every number ending in 2 counts as called. Games become much shorter.
- **Hard-way bingo.** Any pattern played without the FREE square.
- **Second-chance coverall.** After the first blackout, keep calling until another card is covered.
- **U-pick-'em bingo.** Players choose which numbers to play from cards that hold all 75.
- **Word and picture bingo.** Teachers replace numbers with words, pictures or sums, a use Wikipedia notes in American schools and English classes.
