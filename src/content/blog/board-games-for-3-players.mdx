Three players is the most common game night that box covers forget about. Many favorites were built for four, and a few were built for two, so the third player ends up as the kingmaker or the target. This list keeps only board games that **BoardGameGeek players vote best with exactly three**, explains why three works in each one, and ends with four free games you can print before the others arrive.

If your three-player night is a deck of cards rather than a box, our [card games for 3 people](/blog/card-games-for-3-people/) covers Hearts, Cribbage, cut-throat Spades and seven more.

## Why is three players tricky for board games?

Two things go wrong with three. The first is **two against one**: in any game where you can attack another player, the two trailing players have every reason to gang up on the leader, and the leader cannot fight both. The second is **kingmaking**: near the end, the player who can no longer win often decides which of the other two does.

The games that shine with three share one trait: **you mostly compete with the board, not with a chosen opponent**. You race for the same cards, tiles or spaces on a shared map, or you build on your own player board. Interaction stays indirect, so nobody can be singled out. Every pick below fits that pattern, and the free games at the end do too.

## Light board games for three players

These six take under an hour, teach in ten minutes and suit families with children from about 8 or 10.

### Splendor

Marc André's game of Renaissance gem merchants (Space Cowboys, 2014) is our first pick for a three-player table. On your turn you take gem chips, buy a card, or reserve one; cards give permanent discounts and points, and the first to 15 prestige points ends the game. **The publisher's rules scale it for three**: you reveal 4 noble tiles instead of 5 and play with 5 chips of each gem color instead of 7, so the stacks run short and every grab matters. The only way to hurt an opponent is to take the card or chips they wanted.

### Takenoko

Antoine Bauza's panda game (2011) has everyone tending one shared bamboo garden, moving the same gardener and the same hungry panda to complete their own objective cards. Because the pieces are shared, each move helps or hinders the others by accident rather than by attack, which is exactly what three players need. BoardGameGeek lists 8 and up and about 45 minutes.

### Hey, That's My Fish!

The shortest game here: 20 minutes on an ice field of hexagonal tiles. Each turn you slide one penguin in a straight line and take the tile it started on, so **the ice shrinks and penguins get stranded**. Designed by Günter Cornett and Alvydas Jakeliunas (2003), it is a pure strategy game with no luck once the tiles are laid out. With three, everyone is fighting over the same shrinking board, so nobody is left out.

### Akropolis and Cascadia

Both are tile-laying games where **you build your own area** and only compete for the tiles on offer. In Jules Messaud's Akropolis (2022), you take a tile from a shared construction site and stack districts on several levels of your Greek city. In Randy Flynn's Cascadia (2021), each pick pairs a habitat tile with a wildlife token, and you score for animal patterns set by the scoring cards and for your largest habitat areas. Akropolis is voted best at three; Cascadia best at two or three.

### Ticket to Ride: Nordic Countries

Alan R. Moon's standard Ticket to Ride is recommended at three but voted best at four. **Nordic Countries (2007) was designed for 2 or 3 players only**, on a tighter Scandinavian map with ferries and tunnels. BoardGameGeek's description notes its heavier focus on blocking, which keeps three players in each other's way on a board built for exactly that number.

### Wingspan and Everdell: build your own engine

Elizabeth Hargrave's Wingspan (Stonemaier Games, 2019) is an engine-building board game (each card you play makes your later turns stronger) with birds in three habitats; the most points after four rounds wins. Everdell (James A. Wilson, 2018) mixes worker placement (sending pieces to board spaces to take actions) with a city of up to 15 woodland cards. In both, **most of the action happens in front of you**, and the only friction is a card or a space someone else took first. At three, downtime stays short.

### Lost Ruins of Arnak

Elwen and Mín's game (Czech Games Edition, 2020) combines deck-building (you improve a small deck of cards as you play) with worker placement on an island map. You take one action per turn, so turns are quick, and with three players the shared action spaces stay contested without long waits between turns. Playing time on BoardGameGeek runs from 30 to 120 minutes, so expect the long end on a first game.

### Terraforming Mars and Great Western Trail

The two heaviest competitive picks. In Jacob Fryxelius's Terraforming Mars (FryxGames, 2016), corporations raise Mars's temperature, oxygen and oceans while racing for points from over two hundred project cards; its BoardGameGeek poll has the most votes of any game here. Alexander Pfister's Great Western Trail (2016) has you driving cattle from Texas to Kansas City and using buildings along the trail. Both are long (BoardGameGeek lists 120 minutes for Terraforming Mars and up to 150 for Great Western Trail), and **both keep three players busy with their own plans** rather than with each other.

### Gloomhaven: a cooperative pick

Three is a good number for a co-op too, since there is no third wheel when everyone is on the same side. Isaac Childres's Gloomhaven (2017) is a campaign of tactical fights against the game's monsters, played scenario after scenario, and it is voted best at three. It is a big commitment, rated 14 and up. For lighter games where you win or lose together, see our guide to [cooperative board games](/blog/cooperative-board-games/).

## Which famous games are not at their best with three?

Some of the best-selling board games play with three but are voted better at another count. **Catan** and the original **Ticket to Ride** are voted best with four (both recommended at three). **Carcassonne** and **Azul** are voted best with two. They still work at three; they are just not the games to buy for a three-player group. If you love Catan and want a game that suits three better, our [games like Catan](/blog/games-like-catan/) list has options.

## Free board games to print for three

No box on the shelf? These four are free, and two of them are voted best at three on BoardGameGeek.

**Chinese checkers** is the oldest game on this list and **voted best with three**. Each player fills one point of the star and races ten pieces across, hopping over any piece in the way; nothing is captured. With three, each player takes a point with an empty point opposite, as our [Chinese checkers rules](/rules/chinese-checkers/) explain. The printable set puts the star and the pieces on two Letter pages.

<ItemCallout href="/board-games/printable-chinese-checkers/" note="The star board and coloured cut-out pieces on two Letter pages. 2 to 6 players, voted best at three on BGG." />

**Village Pillage** (Jellybean Games, 2019) is a card game that is almost made for three. Every round you secretly play one card toward each neighbour, then all cards flip at once. **With three players, your two neighbours are the other two players**, so everyone faces everyone every round and nobody can be ignored. Voters rate it best at three or four, and Jellybean gives 10 to 15 minutes a game. Jellybean lets us host the full-art cards.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/village-pillage/" note="Farmers, Walls, Raiders and Merchants: first village to three relics wins. 2 to 5 players, free to print." />

**The Carcassonne print-and-play demo** from Asmodee lets you try Klaus-Jürgen Wrede's tile-laying classic for free. Voters rate Carcassonne best with two, but recommend it at every count from two to five, and it is one of the easiest games to teach a family.

<ItemCallout href="/board-games/carcassonne-print-and-play-demo/" note="Free land tiles and demo rules from Asmodee's Print & Play site. 2 to 5 players, ages 7 and up." />

**Goblin Teeth** is a dice-bidding game from Jellybean Games for 2 to 5: roll three dice, bid them on goblin treasures without tying or going over ten, and cheat when you can. Its BoardGameGeek poll has only a handful of votes (best at four, recommended at three), so treat it as a fun extra rather than a three-player specialist.

The free [Citadels demo](/card-games/citadels-print-and-play-demo/) seats 2 to 8, and BoardGameGeek voters do recommend the game at three, but they rate it best at five. Keep it for a night when more friends turn up.
</Callout>
