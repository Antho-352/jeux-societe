The best board game for adults depends on the evening: a loud party, a night of lying to your friends, a long strategy game, a team effort or a quiet game for two. Below are 20 games sorted by those five nights, each with its players, time and BoardGameGeek weight, and a free print-and-play alternative where one exists. A quiz at the end picks one for your table.

## How did we pick, and what does "for adults" mean?

**"For adults" here means games that reward grown-up players**: bluffing, reading people, long-term planning, clever clues. It does not mean adult content. Most of these games are listed for ages 10 and up; a few, like Brass: Birmingham and Spirit Island, are simply too long and deep for most children.

Every number comes from BoardGameGeek, read on 19 October 2026. **Weight** is BoardGameGeek's complexity score, voted by its users from 1 (light) to 5 (heavy). We left out the games where luck decides everything and the ones that need a whole weekend.

## What are the best party games for adults?

**Party games for adults trade luck for wit**: the fun is in a clue that is clever without being obscure.

**Codenames** (Vlaada Chvátil, Czech Games Edition, 2015) is the safe first pick. Two spymasters give one-word clues with a number ("planet, 2") so their team can find its agents among 25 words, without touching the assassin. It plays 2 to 8 in 15 minutes, and extra guests just join a team.

**Wavelength** (Alex Hague, Justin Vickers and Wolfgang Warsch, 2019) asks a teammate to place a clue on a scale between two opposites, such as hot and cold ("salad" for slightly cold). The team then argues about where to turn the dial, which is where the evening goes. It is the lightest game here (1.10) and takes up to 12.

**Decrypto** (Thomas Dagenais-Lespérance, Scorpion Masqué, 2018) is Codenames with a counter-spy. Each team has four secret words numbered 1 to 4; every round one player gets a code like 4-2-1 and clues it for their team, while the other team listens round after round to break the pattern. Voters rate it best at 4 or 6.

**Dixit** (Jean-Louis Roubira, Libellud, 2008) replaces words with dreamlike pictures. The storyteller hints at one card; if everyone finds it, or no one does, the storyteller scores nothing. It works best with 5 or 6.

## Which bluffing and deduction games are best for adults?

**Hidden-role games are where adults shine**, because the game is reading faces and arguing.

- **Secret Hitler** (Mike Boxleiter, Tommy Maranges and Max Temkin, 2016): liberals and hidden fascists elect governments that pass laws drawn from a random deck. Was that fascist law bad luck or betrayal? 5 to 10 players, 45 minutes, free to print under a Creative Commons licence.
- **The Resistance: Avalon** (Don Eskridge, Indie Boards & Cards, 2012): the same tension with knights and quests. Merlin knows who is evil but must not be found out. 5 to 10 players, 30 minutes.
- **Coup** (Rikki Tahta, Indie Boards & Cards, 2012): two face-down character cards each, and you may claim any character's power, whether you hold it or not. Get caught bluffing and you lose a card. 2 to 6 players, 15 minutes.
- **Blood on the Clocktower** (Steven Medway, The Pandemonium Institute, 2022): a deep werewolf game run by a Storyteller for 5 to 20 players, where every character has an ability and the dead keep playing as ghosts. The heaviest party game on BoardGameGeek's scale here, at 3.02.

## What are the best strategy board games for adults?

**Start light and climb.** The six games below are in order of weight, so you can pick the step that suits your group.

**Ticket to Ride** (Alan R. Moon, Days of Wonder, 2004) is the gateway. In the designer's words, each turn you "either draw more cards, claim a route, or get additional Destination Tickets"; longer routes and completed tickets score. 2 to 5 players, 30 to 60 minutes.

**Cascadia** (Randy Flynn, Flatout Games, 2021) is a calm puzzle about the Pacific Northwest. Each turn you take a habitat tile paired with a wildlife token, add the tile to your map and place the animal where it scores. 1 to 4 players, 30 to 45 minutes.

**Citadels** (Bruno Faidutti, 2000) mixes strategy with bluffing. Each round you secretly draft a character, such as the Assassin, the Thief or the King, then build districts in your city. Because roles change hands every round, the leader is always a target. 2 to 8 players.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/citadels-print-and-play-demo/" note="Asmodee's free Citadels demo: character and district cards plus shortened rules, from age 10." />

**Wingspan** (Elizabeth Hargrave, Stonemaier Games, 2019) is an engine-building game (each card you play makes your later turns stronger) about birds. Every bird you add to one of three habitats improves an action: gaining food, laying eggs or drawing cards. 1 to 5 players, 40 to 70 minutes.

**Terraforming Mars** (Jacob Fryxelius, FryxGames, 2016) gives each player a corporation working to raise the planet's temperature, oxygen and ocean coverage, while competing for points with more than 200 project cards. Plan on two hours.

**Brass: Birmingham** (Gavan Brown, Matt Tolman and Martin Wallace, Roxley, 2018) is the deepest pick: rival entrepreneurs build industries and networks over a canal era and a rail era between 1770 and 1870. It weighs 3.86 and is listed for ages 14 and up. If Catan is your group's reference, our [games like Catan](/blog/games-like-catan/) and [games like Risk](/blog/games-like-risk/) lists go further.

## Which cooperative games are best for adults?

**Co-ops suit groups that would rather beat the game than each other.**

- **Pandemic** (Matt Leacock, Z-Man Games, 2008): a team of specialists races to cure four diseases while outbreaks spread. 2 to 4 players, 45 minutes.
- **The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine** (Thomas Sing, KOSMOS, 2019): a cooperative trick-taking game (each round, the highest card wins the cards played) with 50 missions, where you may say very little about your hand. 2 to 5 players, 20 minutes.
- **Spirit Island** (R. Eric Reuss, Greater Than Games, 2017): spirits of the land drive colonizing invaders off an island. At 4.08, it is the heaviest game in this guide.

## What are the best board games for two adults?

**These three are made for exactly two** and fit in half an hour.

**7 Wonders Duel** (Antoine Bauza and Bruno Cathala, Repos Production, 2015) has you take cards from a shared layout over three ages, and you can win by points, by military or by science. **Sky Team** (Luc Rémond, Scorpion Masqué, 2023) is cooperative: a pilot and co-pilot land a plane by placing dice without talking. **Jaipur** (Sébastien Pauchon, 2009) is a quick trading duel: take or sell goods each turn, and keep an eye on the camels.

For a free evening for two, [The Last Trick-or-Treat](/escape-games/the-last-trick-or-treat/) is a printable two-player escape room, and our [board games for couples](/blog/board-games-for-couples/) post has 15 more picks.

## Which board games for adults can you print for free?

**Five official files let you try a grown-up game tonight**: Secret Hitler, and the Dixit, Citadels and Pandemic: Hot Zone demos above, plus one full game.

**Unfair** (Joel Finch, 2am Gameworks, 2017) is a theme-park building card game for 2 to 5 players aged 14 and up. Over eight rounds you build attractions and upgrades, chase secret blueprints and play events against rival parks. The print-and-play is the complete base game for personal use, not a demo: six theme packs, the board and tokens.
