Cooperative board games end the argument about who won: the whole table wins, or the whole table loses. That makes them a good fit for families with a sore loser, couples, and groups who want to talk to each other rather than attack each other. Below are 15 co-op games sorted by who is playing, each with its players, ages and time from BoardGameGeek, plus the free co-op games in our catalogue that you can print tonight. A short quiz at the end picks one for your table.

## What is a cooperative board game?

**A cooperative board game puts every player on the same team, playing against the game itself.** The game fights back with a system that gets worse every turn: diseases spread in Pandemic, the island floods in Forbidden Island, the sun rises in Hoot Owl Hoot!. You share one goal, one clock and one result.

A few games are **semi-cooperative**: the team can lose together, but players also have private goals. Bloc by Bloc: Uprising, free in our catalogue, works this way. Many co-ops can also be played solo, because one player can run the whole team.

## Which cooperative board games are best for families?

**Pick a co-op where everything is out in the open**, so a parent can help without taking over.

**Hoot Owl Hoot!** (Susan McKinley Ross, Peaceable Kingdom, 2010) is the youngest game here, listed for ages 4 and up. Everyone keeps their cards face up. On your turn you play a color card to fly an owl to the next space of that color; if you hold a sun card, you must play it instead, and the sun moves closer to dawn. Get every owl to the nest before sunrise and the team wins.

**Outfoxed!** (Gamewright, 2014) is a whodunit for ages 5 and up: a fox has stolen a pot pie. You roll dice to move and find clues, then check suspects with an evidence scanner, while the thief runs for the exit. It plays 2 to 4 in about 20 minutes.

**Bandido** (Martin Nedergaard Andersen, Helvetiq) is a cooperative card game about blocking tunnels. In Helvetiq's free Covid-19 edition, you lay tunnel cards together to close every exit before the virus escapes.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/bandido-covid-19-edition/" note="33 tunnel cards and a Super Card to print on card stock. 1 to 4 players from age 6, about 15 minutes." />

**Forbidden Island** (Matt Leacock, Gamewright, 2010) is the step up. The island is built from tiles that sink as you play. Collect the four treasures, gather everyone at Fools' Landing and fly off with a Helicopter Lift card before the water rises too far. BoardGameGeek lists it for 2 to 4 players aged 10 and up, about 30 minutes.

## Which co-op games work best for two players?

**Sky Team** (Luc Rémond, Scorpion Masqué, 2023) is made only for two. One of you is the pilot, the other the co-pilot, and you land an airliner by placing dice in the cockpit **without talking**: balance the plane, control speed, lower the flaps and the landing gear. Each airport adds new problems. It plays in about 20 minutes, for ages 10 and up.

**Pandemic** (Matt Leacock, Z-Man Games, 2008) is the classic team-of-specialists co-op. Each player has a role with a special ability; you travel the world, treat disease and collect cards of one color to discover cures, while outbreaks spread. Cure all four diseases and you win. It is listed for 2 to 4 players, 8 and up, 45 minutes, and BoardGameGeek players recommend it at every count from one to four.

## Which cooperative games suit a bigger group?

**These four work at a party table and teach in five minutes.** Three of them, Hanabi, The Mind and The Crew, are cooperative card games.

- **Just One** (Ludovic Roudy and Bruno Sautter, Repos Production, 2018) is a word game for 3 to 7. Everyone but the guesser writes a one-word clue for a secret word; identical clues are removed before the guesser sees them, so you try to be original. BoardGameGeek players vote 6 or 7 as the best count.
- **Hanabi** (Antoine Bauza, 2010) has you hold your cards facing outward: you see everyone's hand but your own. You give hints about colors or numbers to help the team play fireworks in order, 1 to 5, in each color. For 2 to 5 players, 25 minutes.
- **The Mind** (Wolfgang Warsch, 2018) uses cards numbered 1 to 100. The team must play everyone's cards in rising order without speaking at all. Each level deals one more card per player. For 2 to 4 players, 20 minutes.
- **The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine** (Thomas Sing, KOSMOS, 2019) is a cooperative trick-taking game (each round, the highest card wins the cards played) with 50 missions. Each player must win particular tricks, and you may say very little about your hand. For 2 to 5, 20 minutes a mission.

## How do co-op games stop one player from running everything?

**This is the "alpha player" problem**, also called quarterbacking: in a game where all information is open, the most experienced player can work out everyone's best move and simply tell them. The others stop playing and start obeying.

Good co-op designs get around it by limiting what you can share:

| Technique | How it works | Games |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden hands | You cannot see your own cards, or only you see yours | Hanabi, The Crew |
| No talking | Turns are played in silence | The Mind, Sky Team |
| Simultaneous turns | Everyone chooses at the same time | Spirit Island |
| Secret clues | Each player writes alone, then everyone reveals | Just One |

With open games like Pandemic or Forbidden Island, a simple house rule helps: **the player whose turn it is has the final word**, and advice is offered only when asked. With young children, an open game is a feature, not a flaw: in Hoot Owl Hoot! the adult can explain each choice out loud.

**Spirit Island** (R. Eric Reuss, Greater Than Games, 2017) casts you as spirits of the land defending an island from colonizing invaders. Every turn the players choose their power cards at the same time, and fast powers resolve before the invaders spread. It is the heaviest game here, for 1 to 4 players aged 13 and up, 90 to 120 minutes, and BoardGameGeek voters rate it best at two.

**Gloomhaven** (Isaac Childres, Cephalofair Games, 2017) is a campaign of tactical fights with cards instead of dice for your actions. Between scenarios your choices steer a branching story, and your characters gain experience and loot. For 1 to 4 players, 14 and up, 60 to 120 minutes a scenario.

**Pandemic Legacy: Season 1** (Rob Daviau and Matt Leacock, Z-Man Games, 2015) starts as ordinary Pandemic and changes for good as you play. The campaign runs 12 to 24 sessions of about an hour, depending on how well your group does.

## Are escape room games cooperative?

**Yes: an escape room game is a cooperative puzzle.** The whole team solves riddles and codes against the clock, and nobody scores alone.

**Unlock!** (Space Cowboys, 2017) uses numbered cards and a free companion app that gives hints, checks codes and keeps time. You search scenes and combine objects by adding card numbers. **Exit: The Game** (Inka and Markus Brand, KOSMOS) is a series of boxed stories; in The Pharaoh's Tomb (2016) you are locked in a burial chamber with a notebook and a spinning code dial. BoardGameGeek voters rate both best with two players.
