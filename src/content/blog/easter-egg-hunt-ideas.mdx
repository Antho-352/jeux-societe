An Easter egg hunt is simple: hide eggs, let the children find them. Making it fun for a 2-year-old, a 9-year-old and a teenager on the same morning is harder. Below are 20 Easter egg hunt ideas sorted by age, starting with the fairness tricks that stop the big kids from sweeping the lawn, then twists for toddlers, kids, teens and adults, indoor versions for a rainy day, and games to play once the baskets are full.

## Making the hunt fair for different ages

**Most Easter morning tears come from one child finding everything.** Wikipedia describes the egg hunt as a game in which decorated eggs (hard-boiled, chocolate, or plastic eggs filled with candy) are hidden indoors or outdoors for children to find, and notes that the eggs often vary in size. Use that variety to keep things even:

- **One color per child.** Each child collects only their color: blue eggs for one, yellow for another. Everyone ends with the same number, and older children can still enjoy helping.
- **Height zones.** Hide toddler eggs low and in plain sight, school-age eggs at knee to waist height, and teen eggs high or inside things.
- **A head start.** Let the youngest go first; send the others a minute or two later.
- **A quota.** Everyone stops at a set number of eggs, then helps someone who has fewer.
- **Count before you hide.** Write the total down. A forgotten real egg in the garden is not a nice find in May.

## Easter egg hunt ideas for toddlers

**For children under 4, finding is the fun; searching is not.** Keep it short, in one room or a small patch of lawn.

1. **Plain-sight hunt.** Scatter big, bright plastic eggs on the grass or the living room floor where they can be seen from standing height. Toddlers love the gathering more than the hiding.
2. **Color-match baskets.** Give each toddler a basket or bag in one color and ask them to find only the eggs that match. It doubles as a color game.
3. **Bunny trail.** Lay paper bunny footprints from the door to the eggs, so the youngest can follow the trail on their own.

<Callout type="warning" title="Safe fillings for toddlers">
The American Academy of Pediatrics advises keeping hard, gooey or sticky candy from children until age 4 or older, and lists coins, marbles, small balls and toys with small parts among choking hazards. For toddlers, use large plastic eggs filled with stickers, or no filling at all, and tape the eggs shut.
</Callout>

## Ideas for kids: themes and puzzle eggs

**From about 5 to 9, children want a challenge and a story.** These twists add both.

4. **One color each.** The fairness rule above, used as the game itself: the first to find all of their color wins, then helps the others.
5. **The golden egg.** Hide one gold or glittery egg with a special prize or the final clue. Hide it last and best.
6. **Letter eggs.** Put one letter in each egg. Once all the eggs are found, the children unscramble the letters into a word (BUNNY, SPRING, or the name of the place where the basket is hidden).
7. **A clue trail.** Instead of loose eggs, a chain of rhyming clues leads to the basket. Our [Easter scavenger hunt clues](/blog/easter-scavenger-hunt-clues/) give 40 ready-made clues by age, and the free printable set below has 12 cards for an indoor hunt and 12 for outdoors.
8. **Challenge eggs.** A slip of paper in each egg sets a small task before the egg can go in the basket: hop like a bunny five times, say the alphabet backwards, name three spring flowers.
9. **Sound eggs.** Wikipedia notes that eggs which beep, click or play music have been made so that children with visual impairments can take part in egg hunts. A hunt by sound is fun for everyone: blindfolded players with a sighted helper.

## Easter egg hunts for teens

**Teenagers do not want an easier hunt; they want a harder one.** Darkness, points and codes all work.

10. **Flashlight hunt.** Hunt after dark with flashlights, or put a glow stick inside each plastic egg so it shines through the shell. Set a boundary and a time limit, and keep it to a yard or a few rooms you know well.
11. **Points by color.** Each egg color is worth a different number of points (the rare color scores most). Give the group five minutes and add up the scores.
12. **Team photo challenge.** Each egg holds a photo task (a team pyramid, a selfie with the oldest person at the party, a picture of something yellow). The first team to complete every task wins.
13. **Padlock code.** Each of four eggs holds one digit. The hunters find all four, work out the order (number the eggs or add a riddle), and open a padlocked box with the prize. It is a mini escape room; if your teens like the idea, try one of the free demos on our [escape games](/escape-games/) page, such as [Unlock! The Elite](/escape-games/unlock-demo-the-elite/).

## Adult Easter egg hunt ideas

**For adults, the egg is an excuse for a game.** Keep the prizes small and the rules short.

14. **Cryptic clue trail.** Each egg points to the next hiding place with a cryptic crossword-style clue, such as "Place to sit hidden in also famous (4)" for the sofa. Our [clues article](/blog/easter-scavenger-hunt-clues/) has ten with answers.
15. **Egg swap.** Each egg holds a numbered slip for a wrapped prize. In turn, players open a prize or steal one that has already been opened, as in a gift swap.
16. **Trivia eggs.** Each egg holds a question. Answer correctly to keep the egg; get it wrong and put it back for someone else to find.
17. **Puzzle relay.** Teams find an egg, solve the puzzle inside (a page from our [hard maze](/puzzles/mazes/hard-maze/) or an [easy sudoku](/puzzles/sudoku/easy-sudoku/)), and only then get the clue to the next egg.

## Indoor Easter egg hunt ideas for rainy days

**An indoor hunt needs smaller hiding places and a clearer plan.** Close the doors to rooms that are off limits before you start.

18. **Treasure map.** Draw a simple floor plan of the house with an X for each hidden egg. Children read the map to find them; older ones get a map without the X's and a list of clues.
19. **Hot and cold.** One adult guides each searcher with "warmer" and "colder" until the egg is found. It works with only a handful of eggs and in a small apartment.
20. **Puzzle-piece eggs.** Print a puzzle, cut it into 6 to 8 pieces and put one in each egg. Once every piece is found, the children tape the page together and solve it. ## Games to play after the hunt

**Once the baskets are full, a calm game keeps the chocolate from disappearing all at once.**

- **Easter bingo.** Our [Easter bingo cards](/bingo/easter-bingo/) use 36 spring words instead of numbers (bunny, egg hunt, daffodil, hot cross bun), with 20 different cards and calling cards to cut out; they suit children from about 6. The [bingo patterns guide](/blog/bingo-patterns/) suggests quick patterns for young players.
- **Egg roll.** At the White House Easter Egg Roll, children push an egg across the grass with a long-handled spoon. Wikipedia dates the event at the White House to 1878, when President Rutherford B. Hayes and Lucy Hayes brought it there at the request of children. Mark a start and finish line and race with hard-boiled eggs (not for eating afterwards).
- **Egg and spoon race.** Carry an egg on a spoon to the finish line without dropping it; plastic eggs make it easier for the youngest.
- **Cascarones.** In this Latin American tradition, now shared by many US states, emptied eggshells are filled with confetti, sealed with tissue paper and cracked over each other's heads, as Wikipedia describes. Do it outside.
- **Quiet puzzles.** An [Easter word search](/puzzles/word-search/easter-word-search/) or the [Easter maze](/puzzles/mazes/easter-maze/) for a few calm minutes while the adults tidy up.
