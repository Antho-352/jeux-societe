Exploding Kittens is the game people ask for again the moment someone explodes. Once the jokes on the cards are familiar, though, the table wants the same rush with something new. The twelve games below keep what makes it work, the short, mean, laugh-out-loud turns, and change the rest. Six are commercial games, with player counts and ages from BoardGameGeek; six are free games from Jellybean Games you can print tonight. A quiz at the end picks one for your group.

## Why is Exploding Kittens so much fun?

**Every draw is a risk, and every card in your hand is a way out.** Players take turns drawing from one deck until someone pulls an Exploding Kitten and loses. BoardGameGeek calls it "a kitty-powered version of Russian Roulette". The other cards let you peek at the deck, shuffle it or force another player to draw more; the publisher sums it up as action cards to "skip, attack, shuffle, or defuse your way to being the last player standing".

**The tension rises on its own.** The deck holds one Exploding Kitten fewer than the number of players, so as it thins out, each draw gets more dangerous. A Defuse card saves you once. That is the loop to look for in a game "like Exploding Kittens": quick turns, cards that hurt other players, and a way to dodge at the last second.

## Which games have the same attack-and-cancel tension?

**These two keep the heart of Exploding Kittens: cards that hurt someone, and cards that stop them.** Both are by Ramy Badie.

**Unstable Unicorns** (2017) is the closest. You draw and discard cards in a race to build an army of seven unicorns, while everyone else plays Downgrade cards to stop you. Then there are Neigh cards ("Get it? Neigh?", asks the publisher), held back to stop a rival's play in its tracks, the same last-second answer Exploding Kittens players keep in hand. It plays two to eight from age 8, in 30 to 60 minutes, so it also works when your group outgrows Exploding Kittens' five seats.

**Here to Slay** (2020) is the same team's fantasy follow-up, published by TeeTurtle and Unstable Games. You pick a party leader, recruit heroes and roll two dice to use their powers or slay monsters; the first to slay three monsters, or to gather six hero classes, wins. When a rival plays a card you dislike, a Challenge card forces a dice duel; win it and their card goes straight to the discard pile. Two to six players, from age 10, 30 to 60 minutes. The dice add the "will it blow up?" feeling of a risky draw.

## Which take-that games work for families?

**For a mixed table of kids and adults, these three swap the bomb for food, slapping and flying burritos.** None takes more than half an hour.

**Sushi Go!** (Phil Walker-Harding, 2013) is the gentle one. Everyone picks one card from their hand and passes the rest, so you grab the best sushi combinations as they go round. BoardGameGeek's description warns: "be careful which sushi you allow your friends to take." The meanness is in what you keep away from others, not in attacks. Two to five players, 15 minutes, ages 8 and up.

**Taco Cat Goat Cheese Pizza** (Dave Campbell, 2018) is pure noise. Players lay cards on a shared pile while saying the next word of "taco, cat, goat, cheese, pizza"; when card and word match, everyone slaps the pile, and the last hand down takes it. Gorilla, narwhal and groundhog cards add a gesture before the slap. Empty your hand first to win. Two to eight players, from age 8.

**Throw Throw Burrito** (2019) comes from Matthew Inman and Elan Lee, two of the three Exploding Kittens designers, with Brian S. Spence. BoardGameGeek calls it "the world's first dodgeball card game": you race to collect sets of three cards, and burrito cards start battles where players throw squishy toy burritos at each other. A hit costs points, and a Burrito Duel puts two players back to back for three paces. Two to six players, 15 minutes, ages 7 and up. Clear the table of anything breakable.

## What about bluffing games for teens and adults?

**Coup** (Rikki Tahta, 2012) turns Exploding Kittens' "last player standing" into a game of lies. Each player has two face-down character cards from a deck of fifteen (three each of five characters) and two coins. On your turn you may claim any character's power: the Duke takes three coins, the Assassin pays three to kill a card, the Captain steals two. Nobody has to tell the truth, but anyone can challenge. Caught lying, you lose a card; challenge an honest player and you lose one instead. Lose both cards and you are out. Two to six players, 15 minutes, ages 13 and up.

**[Meow!](/card-games/meow/)** is the free party pick. Jellybean Games' version of Mao puts every secret rule on a card; you play a rule, act out the ones you think other players hold, then ask whether you broke any. It is loud and physical, with hissing and pretend paw-licking, and seats 3 to 12 from age 6.

## Which free card games like Exploding Kittens can you print?

**Jellybean Games gives away complete games, and five of them have the quick, nasty turns Exploding Kittens fans like.** Each is listed with players, age and time from the publisher's files; several are hosted on our pages with the publisher's permission.

**Scuttle!** (Peter C. Hayward, 2016) is a pirate race to 21 doubloons. On your turn you draw a card or play one, as treasure or for its action: steal a treasure chest, wipe the board, or set a parrot or a monkey to guard your loot. Turns take seconds, and the solo mode against a "Robot Pirate" lets kids learn it alone.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/scuttle/" note="Cards and a colour rulebook for 1 to 5 players, ages 6 and up. Print on card stock and cut." />

**Ninjitsu!** (2018) is its sequel and the closest to Exploding Kittens' hidden danger: any card can be played face down as a secret. Opponents can spend a turn stealing it, and some secrets are booby-trapped.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/ninjitsu/" note="A 41-card deck and a short rulebook, free on the publisher's site. 2 to 5 players, 5 to 10 minutes." />

**Brains!** (2019) adds zombies and a graveyard: bury the top card of the deck for an ongoing effect, or play cards as treasure and attacks. Its deck can be shuffled with Scuttle! and Ninjitsu!.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/brains/" note="44 cards, a gravestone card and a 12-page rulebook with a sample game. 2 to 5 players, ages 6 and up." />

**Village Pillage** (Tom Lang and Peter C. Hayward, 2019) is for slightly older players. Everyone secretly plays one card towards each neighbour, then all cards flip at once: Farmers grow turnips, Raiders steal them, Walls block, Merchants buy relics. Three relics wins. It is two rock-paper-scissors guesses per round, 10 to 15 minutes, from age 10.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/village-pillage/" note="The full-art cards are hosted here; the rulebook and the Surf & Turf expansion are on Jellybean Games' site. 2 to 5 players." />

**Goblin Teeth** (Frank Tedeschi, 2020) brings the chaos to dice. You bid dice on items without passing ten or tying another goblin, and Cheat cards let you reroll, move or steal dice to wreck a rival's bid. It runs 20 to 30 minutes, for two to five players from age 8; you supply 15 dice.

<ItemCallout href="/dice-games/goblin-teeth/" note="Rulebook and mats hosted here; the 66 cards are a separate download from Jellybean Games. 2 to 5 players." />
