Most board games for couples lists rank the same boxes from best to worst. That misses the point: a couple that bickers over every point needs a different game from one that wants a puzzle to solve together, and a Tuesday at 9 pm is not a Saturday. Below are fifteen games sorted by the kind of date night you want, with time, weight and awards from BoardGameGeek, and seven of them you can print free tonight. A quiz at the end matches you to one.

## What makes a good board game for couples?

**It is built for two.** Many family games technically allow two players but feel empty without a third, which is why Catan, for example, starts at three. Games designed for exactly two give each of you something to do on every turn and never leave one of you waiting.

Three more things matter on a date night:

- **Compete or cooperate.** If one of you hates losing to the other, choose a cooperative game: you both win or both lose.
- **Length.** A weeknight game should end in 15 to 30 minutes. Save the 45-minute games for a free evening.
- **Talk.** Some games are quiet duels of calculation; others, like Choose Two or Codenames: Duet, are really about talking. Pick for the mood you want.

## Which quick games work on a weeknight?

These four take 30 minutes or less and are designed for exactly two players.

**Patchwork** is Uwe Rosenberg's quilt-building game (Lookout Games, 2014). You buy fabric patches with buttons and fit them on your own 9-by-9 board like a puzzle. Each patch also costs time on a shared track, and whoever is behind on the track plays next, so a cheap, quick patch can buy you two turns in a row. Empty squares cost two points each at the end. It won BoardGameGeek's Golden Geek for best abstract game in 2014.

**Jaipur** is Sébastien Pauchon's trading duel (2009). You take cards from a shared market or swap several of yours for them, then sell goods to earn tokens that lose value as the game goes on. Sell early for the best tokens, or wait for the bonus on a set of three, four or five. Camels look useless until you need them to swap.

**Lost Cities** is Reiner Knizia's card game of expeditions (KOSMOS, 1999). Every expedition you start costs 20 points, and cards must go down in ascending order, so each new colour is a bet. Handshake cards double, triple or quadruple an expedition's score, whether it ends in profit or loss. A full game is three rounds.

**The Fox in the Forest** is trick-taking for two (each round, the highest card of the suit led, or a trump, wins the cards played). Joshua Buergel's game (Foxtrot Games, 2017) adds characters such as the Fox and the Witch, whose powers change the trump or let you lead after losing a trick. The catch: win too many tricks and you score badly.

## Which cooperative games can couples play together?

**Cooperative games put you on the same side against the game.** Nobody gloats, nobody sulks, and the conversation afterwards is about the game, not about who won.

**Sky Team** is the standout. In Luc Rémond's game (Scorpion Masqué, 2023), one of you is the pilot and the other the co-pilot, landing an airliner by silently placing dice on the cockpit controls: speed, axis, flaps, landing gear, radio. You cannot discuss your dice, so you learn to read each other. Each airport adds new challenges. It won the Spiel des Jahres in 2024 and the Golden Geek for best two-player and best cooperative game for 2023.

**Codenames: Duet** turns the party word game into a game for two. In Vlaada Chvátil and Scot Eaton's version (Czech Games Edition, 2017), 25 words sit in a grid and each of you sees a different key. You take turns giving one-word clues to help the other find your agents, and together you must find all fifteen before time runs out, without hitting an assassin. It won the 2017 Golden Geek for best two-player game.

**Pandemic** works well with two, and you can try it tonight. Z-Man Games gives away the full print-and-play of Pandemic: Hot Zone – North America, a shorter Pandemic with three diseases and a smaller map: on each turn you take four actions to travel, treat cities and share knowledge, then the infection spreads.

## Which deeper games fill a long evening?

When you have the whole evening, these three give you more to plan, and still finish in under an hour.

**7 Wonders Duel** is the two-player version of 7 Wonders, by Antoine Bauza and Bruno Cathala (Repos Production, 2015). Over three ages you take cards from a shared layout to build resources, wonders, armies and science. You can win on points at the end, or at once by marching on your partner's capital or collecting six different science symbols. It won the Golden Geek for best two-player game in 2015.

**Splendor Duel** is Marc André and Bruno Cathala's two-player take on Splendor (Space Cowboys, 2022). You draw gems and pearls from a shared board to buy cards that make future cards cheaper. Its BoardGameGeek description calls it more complex, interactive and "mean" than the original, and there are three ways to win. It also took the Golden Geek for best two-player game.

**Carcassonne** seats two to five, but BoardGameGeek voters rate two as its best count. You lay landscape tiles to build roads, cities and monasteries, and claim them with your followers. With two, every tile you draw is a choice between growing your city and blocking your partner's. Asmodee's free demo lets you try it first.

If you both enjoyed Catan with friends, our guide to [games like Catan](/blog/games-like-catan/) covers Rivals for Catan, its two-player version.

## How do you do an escape room for two at home?

**Print one.** A home escape room is a cooperative puzzle with a story: you search pictures, combine clues and enter codes before time runs out.

**Unlock!** is Space Cowboys' escape-room card game, designed by Cyril Demaegd. The free Temple of Ra demo is 24 cards: you sneak into a freshly excavated temple at night and look for its treasure. The free Unlock! app runs the timer, hints and penalties. You need a colour printer.

<ItemCallout href="/escape-games/unlock-demo-the-temple-of-ra/" note="24 cards on 8 pages, for 1 to 6 players from age 10. Print without reading the cards to avoid spoilers." />

**The Last Trick-or-Treat** from CouchEscape is built for exactly two: one of you goes into a haunted house, the other stays on the street, and you each have different pages. No lock opens unless you describe what you see to each other, which makes it a good test of how you talk under pressure. It is a ghost story for ages 10 and up, with four endings and no timer. For something calmer, [The 23:47 Dispatch](/escape-games/the-23-47-dispatch/) is a 10-to-15-minute Victorian mini-case for one or two, and our [escape games page](/escape-games/) lists more.

## Which games can you print free tonight?

**Choose Two** is the one built for couples. Andrew Looney's free expansion turns the Looney Labs party game Choose One into a getting-to-know-you game for two. You each answer an either-or question and guess your partner's answer, then talk about the difference. Reading your partner better than they read you earns extra cards, and you both win as a couple if the Disagree pile stays smaller than the other two put together. You need the Topic cards from a copy of Choose One.

<ItemCallout href="/party-games/choose-two/" note="4 colour pages from Looney Labs: 8 answer cards, a new board and the rules. Needs Choose One's Topic cards." />

**The Lady and the Tiger** is a set of five microgames in eighteen cards from Jellybean Games (2017), and two of them are made for exactly two. In **Doors**, one of you secretly holds an identity such as Red Lady and collects cards that share its traits, while the other watches what is taken and guesses the colour, the role or both; a wrong guess pays the collector. **Labyrinth** is a two-player maze game with the same cards. Each game takes 10 to 15 minutes, and you add 25 gems or small tokens in five colours.

<ItemCallout href="/card-games/the-lady-and-the-tiger/" note="Five small PDFs with the rules and cards, including Doors and Labyrinth for two. Print on card stock and add gems or tokens." />

**Backgammon** is the oldest duel on this list. Our [printable backgammon board](/board-games/printable-backgammon-board/) prints on two Letter pages with cut-out checkers; add two dice and read our [backgammon rules](/rules/backgammon/) before you start. For more free duels, browse our [print and play games for 2 players](/board-games/2-player/).
