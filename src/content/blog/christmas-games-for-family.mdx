## What games can you play on Christmas Eve?

Christmas Eve is the night children find it hardest to sit still. Games that **involve everyone at once** and end with a small reward work best, because they turn the excitement into something useful.

**1. Christmas bingo.** One card each, one caller drawing words from a hat. Our [Christmas bingo cards](/bingo/christmas-bingo/) are twenty unique 5 × 5 cards built from 36 words (Santa, mistletoe, gingerbread, nutcracker, eggnog and more), two cards per page with a free center square. Page 11 holds the calling cards to cut out. Children from age 6 can play, and adults rarely refuse a round.

**2. Christmas left-right game.** This is the gift-passing game many families play with a story. Everyone sits in a circle, and one wrapped gift (or one per person, for a gift exchange) starts in someone's hands. A reader tells a story; each time the story says "left", the gift moves one place to the left, and each time it says "right", one place to the right. Whoever holds the gift when the story ends keeps it. You need at least four people to make it unpredictable. Here is a story we wrote for it, free to read aloud.

<Callout type="example" title="Left-right story: The gift that wandered off">
On Christmas Eve, Grandma Rose looked **LEFT**, then **RIGHT**. The family gift was gone! She had **LEFT** it on the kitchen table, **RIGHT** next to the cookies.

"Maybe the cat took it," said Uncle Ben, coming in from the yard. But the cat was asleep at the **RIGHT** end of the couch, and that did not seem **RIGHT**.

Little Mia checked the hall closet. She searched the **LEFT** shelf, then the **RIGHT** shelf, and found only boots and a scarf someone had **LEFT** behind last winter.

Dad shook the tree. He lifted the **LEFT** branches, then the **RIGHT** branches, and found a lost candy cane, but no gift.

"**RIGHT**," said Grandma. "Who was the last one in the kitchen?"

Nobody answered **RIGHT** away. Then the dog trotted in from the **LEFT**, tail wagging, a red ribbon stuck to his ear.

"There's our thief!" laughed Mia. They followed him **RIGHT** down the hall, turned **LEFT** at the bathroom, and found the gift under his blanket, not a bite taken out of it.

Grandma read the tag. It was the **RIGHT** gift. "Well," she said, "there is only one thing **LEFT** to do."

She handed it to the person on her **RIGHT**. "Merry Christmas. Hold on tight, and don't let it get away again!"
</Callout>

Every "left" and "right" counts, even in "right away" or "left behind", which is where most of the laughs come from. Read slowly the first time and faster the second. If you know the dice game Left Right Center, this is its storytelling cousin.

**3. Christmas light scavenger hunt.** After dark, pile into the car or put on coats and tick off light displays you spot around the neighborhood. The [Christmas Light Scavenger Hunt by Play Party Plan](/scavenger-hunts/christmas-light-scavenger-hunt-play-party-plan/) is a one-page checklist for 1 to 6 players that takes 30 minutes to an hour. It is free, but the creator sends the PDF after you enter a first name and email address.

## What can kids play on Christmas morning while they wait?

Christmas morning has its own waiting game: for the adults to wake up, for the presents, then for the meal. A **hunt or a paper puzzle** fills that time without adding more toys to the floor.

**4. Christmas clue-card hunt.** Twelve rhyming clue cards send children from the spoon drawer to the couch to the fridge, and the last card points to wherever the toys are kept, where you hide a surprise. The [Christmas scavenger hunt clue cards by Kimberly McLeod](/scavenger-hunts/christmas-scavenger-hunt-clue-cards-best-ideas-for-kids/) are a free direct download (three Letter pages, for personal or school use) for 1 to 4 players. Hide the cards on Christmas Eve after the children are in bed.

**5. Christmas word search.** Twenty Christmas words in a 15 × 15 grid, pitched at ages 7 to 10. In our [Christmas word search](/puzzles/word-search/christmas-word-search/) the words run across, down and diagonally but never backwards, and the answer key is on page 2. Two-word entries such as CANDYCANE are written without a space.

**6. Christmas tree mazes.** Both of our mazes are shaped like a Christmas tree and run from the star to the trunk. The [Christmas maze](/puzzles/mazes/christmas-maze/) is a medium maze for ages 6 to 9 that takes about ten minutes; the [easy Christmas tree maze](/puzzles/mazes/christmas-tree-maze-easy/) has thick walls and a short route for ages 4 to 7. Each has its solution on page 2.

**9. Spoons.** Players pass cards round the circle as fast as they can; whoever collects four of a kind grabs a spoon, and everyone else scrambles for one. There is always one spoon too few. Our [Spoons rules](/rules/spoons/) cover 3 to 13 players with one standard deck.

**10. Left Right Center.** Each player starts with three chips (coins or sweets work). On your turn you roll one die per chip, up to three, and pass chips left, right or into the center as the dice say. The last player holding chips wins the pot. There are no decisions at all, which makes it a favorite with grandparents and five-year-olds alike. The boxed game LCR has special dice, but our [Left Right Center rules](/rules/left-right-center/) show how to play with three ordinary dice.

<Callout type="note" title="Let the youngest lead">
When three generations play together, let the youngest player pick the first charades prompt or roll first at Left Right Center. It sounds small, but it gets the whole table invested in round one.
</Callout>

## What games keep the kids' corner busy?

Sooner or later the children drift to a corner of their own. They need games they can **run without an adult**, quiet enough to play beside a conversation. If you have toddlers too, our guide to [Christmas activities for preschoolers](/blog/christmas-activities-for-preschoolers/) has twenty more ideas by age.

**11. Christmas dot to dots.** Our Christmas dot to dots climb in difficulty, so siblings of different ages can each have one. The youngest start with the [star (10 dots)](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/star-dot-to-dot-easy/) or the [Christmas tree (15 dots)](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/christmas-tree-dot-to-dot/). Children learning their letters can try the [alphabet gingerbread man](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/abc-gingerbread-man-dot-to-dot/), joined with letters instead of numbers. Older kids move on to the [gingerbread man (34 dots)](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/gingerbread-man-dot-to-dot/) and the [snowman (40 dots)](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/snowman-dot-to-dot/). The finished picture is on page 2 of each, ready to color.

**12. Heads, Bodies and Legs.** One child draws a head, folds it over and passes the sheet on; the next draws a body, the next the legs, and nobody sees the result until the end. This [Heads, Bodies and Legs sheet](/party-games/printable-heads-bodies-and-legs-game/) from PrintablePaper.net has flaps to hide each section, for 2 to 6 players. Try a Christmas edition where every creature must be part elf, part reindeer.

**13. Scuttle!** A small pirate card game by Peter C. Hayward: play each card as treasure or for its action, steal your rivals' chests, and be the first to 21 doubloons. [Scuttle!](/card-games/scuttle/) is for 1 to 5 players aged 6 and up, plays in 5 to 10 minutes, and its six card sheets print on card stock. It is a good first "real" card game for a child who got scissors for Christmas.

For two children with a pencil and nothing else, [hangman](/rules/hangman/) with a Christmas word list or [dots and boxes](/rules/dots-and-boxes/) will fill any gap.

## What are the best family games for Christmas night with teens and adults?

Once the children are in bed, teens and adults want something **fast, loud and easy to join**. These are the games that keep a living room laughing until midnight.

**14. Quick on the Draw.** A team drawing game in the Pictionary style: one player draws, their team guesses before the timer runs out. This [Quick on the Draw sheet](/party-games/printable-quick-on-the-draw/) has four team cards with five category rows each, for 4 to 10 players. It has no word list, so write your own on Christmas themes and reuse the one-minute timer above.

**15. Meow!** A party card game of secret rules for 3 to 12 players: everyone holds rules the others cannot see, and you win by taking a turn without breaking any of them. Expect hissing and pretend paw-licking. [Meow!](/card-games/meow/) plays in 5 to 10 minutes from age 6, and its Kitten Cabal deck is the family-friendly version, so it also works earlier in the day.

**16. Love Letter.** Seiji Kanai's tiny game of risk and deduction: hold one card, draw a second, play one, and try to get your letter to the Princess. The [Love Letter print and play](/card-games/love-letter-print-and-play/) is for 2 to 4 players aged 10 and up, about 20 minutes. Asmodee asks you to sign in with a free My asmodee account to download it.

If someone wants a challenge alone, hand them the [reindeer dot to dot](/puzzles/dot-to-dot/reindeer-dot-to-dot-expert/): 141 dots, most of them in the antlers.

## Which Christmas board games for families can you print for free?

None of these is Christmas-themed, but a board game is a classic Christmas present, and these free **print-and-play** versions (files the publisher offers to print at home) let the family try one before buying the box.

**17. Pandemic: Hot Zone – North America.** A short cooperative version of Matt Leacock's Pandemic: the players work together to cure three diseases before outbreaks spread out of control. Everyone wins or loses together, which avoids the Christmas-night sulks. [Pandemic: Hot Zone](/board-games/pandemic-hot-zone-north-america-print-and-play/) is for 2 to 4 players aged 8 and up, about 30 minutes. Z-Man Games gives away the 17-page file; you supply 48 small tokens in three colors and four pawns.

**18. Timeline.** Each card shows an event or invention, with its date on the back; you place it before or after the cards already on the table, then flip it to check. Nobody needs to know exact dates, so children hold their own against adults. The [Timeline Classic demo](/card-games/timeline-classic-print-and-play-demo/) is for 2 to 8 players aged 8 and up, about 15 minutes, and downloads with the same free My asmodee account as Love Letter.

Asmodee also offers free demos of [Carcassonne](/board-games/carcassonne-print-and-play-demo/) (2 to 5 players, age 7 and up) and [Spot It!](/card-games/spot-it-dobble-print-and-play-demo/) (2 to 8 players, age 6 and up), both behind the same free account.
