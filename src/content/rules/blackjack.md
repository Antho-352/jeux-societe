## What you need

A standard 52-card deck (two decks shuffled together for five or more players) and a pile of counters for each player: poker chips, buttons, matchsticks. Blackjack is a casino game, but it works perfectly well at the kitchen table with counters and no money. This page describes that home version; it is not a guide to playing in a casino or for stakes.

### Card values

- 2 to 10: face value
- Jack, queen, king: 10
- Ace: 1 or 11, whichever suits the hand better

A hand that counts an ace as 11 is "soft" (A-6 is a soft 17); any other hand is "hard". An ace plus a ten-value card as your first two cards is a **blackjack** (also called a natural), the best possible hand.

## Setup

Choose a dealer. At home the deal rotates: the dealer changes after every round, or after each player has had a fixed number of rounds, so nobody keeps the dealer's built-in advantage for long. Give everyone the same number of counters, for example 20.

Before each deal, every player except the dealer puts a stake of counters in front of them (one to three is plenty).

## How to play

The dealer gives two cards face up to each player, and takes one card face up and one face down (the "hole card").

If the dealer's face-up card is an ace or a ten, the dealer checks the hole card. A dealer blackjack wins at once against every hand except another blackjack, which is a tie ("push").

### Player options

Starting on the dealer's left, each player in turn decides:

- **Stand.** Keep your total and end your turn.
- **Hit.** Take another card. You may keep hitting until you stand or go over 21 ("bust"), which loses your stake immediately.
- **Double down.** Double your stake, take exactly one more card, then stand. Usually done on 9, 10 or 11.
- **Split.** If your two cards are the same rank, add a second stake and play them as two separate hands, each receiving a new second card. Split aces normally get just one card each.

### The dealer's turn

After every player has finished, the dealer turns over the hole card and must follow fixed rules: draw on 16 or less, stand on 17 or more. Agree beforehand whether the dealer hits or stands on a soft 17; most casinos print the rule on the table, and either is fine at home.

## Scoring and winning

Compare each player's hand with the dealer's:

- Dealer busts: every player still in the hand wins.
- Your total is higher than the dealer's: you win your stake back plus the same amount.
- Blackjack (not after a split): you win one and a half times your stake.
- Equal totals: push, your stake is returned.
- Lower total, or bust: you lose your stake.

Play a set number of rounds, or until an agreed time. The player with the most counters wins. If someone runs out, give them a fresh pile and carry on; it is a game, not a bank.

## Common variants

- **Insurance.** When the dealer shows an ace, players may place a side stake of half their bet that pays 2 to 1 if the dealer has blackjack. Both sources advise against it; at home, leave it out.
- **Five-card Charlie.** A hand of five cards that has not bust wins automatically. A popular family rule.
- **Surrender.** Give up your hand and get half your stake back before taking any other action.
- **Pontoon.** The British cousin, with a "twist" instead of a hit and different payouts. A different game; see pagat.com.
- **No stakes.** For children, skip the counters: each round, every player who beats the dealer scores a point, and the dealer scores a point for each player beaten.

## Tips for first games

- Stand on 12 to 16 when the dealer shows a 2 to 6; the dealer is likely to bust. Hit those totals when the dealer shows 7 or higher.
- Always split aces and 8s; never split 10s.
- Double down on 10 or 11.
- Rotate the dealer and keep the stakes tiny. The dealer wins slightly more often than the players, so the fun is in the decisions, not the pile of counters.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is an ace worth in Blackjack?**
One or eleven, whichever helps you more. A-7 is 18; A-7-9 is 17.

**Does the dealer have to hit on 16?**
Yes. The dealer draws on 16 or less and stands on 17 or more, with a house choice about soft 17.

**What happens on a tie?**
It is a push: nobody wins and your stake comes back to you.

**Is a blackjack better than a three-card 21?**
Yes. An ace and a ten-value card as your first two cards beats any 21 made with more cards, and pays one and a half times the stake.

**Can you play Blackjack without money?**
Yes, and at home you should: use counters, or play for points, and rotate the dealer so everyone gets the dealer's edge in turn.
