## What you need

Two players, two pencils and four 10×10 grids, two for each player. Rows are lettered A to J and columns numbered 1 to 10, so every square has a name like B7. Our printable battleship sheet has both players' grids on one page; cut it in half along the dashed line. A book or a box lid propped up between you keeps the grids hidden.

Battleship was played on paper long before the plastic peg version appeared in 1967. The boxed game is a Hasbro product; the pencil game is free for anyone.

## Setup

On your grid marked **My fleet**, secretly draw your ships by shading squares:

| Ship | Squares |
|---|---|
| Carrier | 5 |
| Battleship | 4 |
| Cruiser | 3 |
| Submarine | 3 |
| Destroyer | 2 |

Each ship sits in a straight line of consecutive squares, horizontally or vertically, never diagonally. Ships may not overlap or hang off the grid. Once play starts, you cannot move them. Your other grid, **My shots**, stays empty: it records where you have fired.

## How to play

1. Decide who starts. Players then take turns, one shot each.
2. On your turn, call one square, for example "D4".
3. Your opponent looks at their fleet grid and answers **"Hit"** if one of their ships covers that square, or **"Miss"** if it does not. In the 1990 Milton Bradley rules they also say which ship was hit.
4. Mark the result on your shots grid: an X for a hit, a dot or slash for a miss. Your opponent marks the hit on their fleet grid.
5. When every square of a ship has been hit, its owner announces **"You sank my cruiser"** (or whichever ship it was).

Every turn is a single shot, hit or miss. Play continues until one fleet is gone.

## Scoring and winning

The first player to sink all five of the opponent's ships wins. For a match, count the ships left afloat on the winner's side, or play best of three with the other player starting each time.

## Common variants

- **Salvo**: on your turn you call five shots at once, and the opponent reports hits after all five. You lose one shot per turn for each of your ships that has been sunk. Recommended in the official rules once players know the basic game.
- **Silent hits**: the defender says "hit" but not which ship, which makes Salvo much harder.
- **Shots equal ships**: you fire one shot for each of your ships still afloat.
- **2002 fleet**: newer Hasbro sets rename the 3-square cruiser a destroyer and add a 2-square patrol boat.
- **Moving ships**: a variant allows each player to move one undamaged ship to an uncalled position every fourth or fifth turn.

## Tips for first games

- Do not cluster your ships. Spread them out and avoid always using the edges.
- Search in a checkerboard pattern: every ship covers at least two squares, so firing at every other square finds them all with half the shots.
- After a hit, try the four neighbouring squares, then follow the line until the ship sinks.
- Once a ship is sunk, cross out the squares around it on your shots grid if your group plays that ships cannot touch.
- Mark your own misses carefully. Calling the same square twice wastes a turn.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can ships touch each other?** In the official rules, yes, as long as they do not overlap. Many families add a "no touching" rule; agree before placing.

**Do you get another shot after a hit?** Not in the standard rules: one shot per turn, hit or miss. Some house rules give an extra shot after a hit.

**Do you have to say which ship was hit?** In the Milton Bradley rules you do. In harder versions you only say "hit".

**Can I play with only one grid?** Yes, but the second grid makes it much easier to track your shots and avoid repeats.

**How many squares do the ships cover in total?** Seventeen, out of the 100 on the grid.
