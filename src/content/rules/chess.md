## What you need

A board of 64 squares, alternately light and dark, and two sets of 16 pieces: a king, a queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns per side. A clock is optional at home. Our printable gives you the board with coordinates and a strip of lettered tokens if you have no set.

## Setup

Place the board so that each player has a light square in their right-hand corner. White's pieces go on the first two ranks (rows): rooks in the corners, then knights, then bishops, with the queen on her own colour (the white queen on the light square d1) and the king beside her on e1. The eight pawns fill the second rank. Black mirrors this on ranks 7 and 8, queen on the dark square d8.

White always moves first. Players then alternate, one move each; passing is not allowed.

## How to play

### How the pieces move

- The **rook** moves any distance along a rank or a file (column).
- The **bishop** moves any distance diagonally, so each bishop stays on one colour for the whole game.
- The **queen** combines both: any distance in a straight line or a diagonal.
- The **king** moves one square in any direction.
- The **knight** moves in an L shape: two squares in one direction, then one square sideways. It is the only piece that may jump over others.
- The **pawn** moves one square straight ahead, or two squares on its first move, and never backwards. It captures differently: one square diagonally forward.

Rooks, bishops and queens cannot pass through other pieces. You capture by moving onto a square occupied by an enemy piece, which is removed from the board. You may never move onto a square occupied by one of your own pieces.

### Three special moves

**Castling** moves the king two squares towards a rook, and the rook jumps to the square the king crossed. It is allowed only if neither piece has moved yet, nothing stands between them, the king is not in check, and the king does not pass through or land on a square attacked by the enemy.

**En passant** ("in passing"): if a pawn advances two squares and lands beside an enemy pawn, that enemy pawn may capture it on the very next move as if it had moved only one square.

**Promotion**: a pawn that reaches the far end of the board is exchanged at once for a queen, rook, bishop or knight of the same colour. Almost everyone chooses a queen, and you may have several.

### Check

A king is in check when an enemy piece attacks it. You must get out of check immediately, by moving the king, capturing the attacker or blocking the line of attack. You may never make a move that leaves your own king in check.

## Scoring and winning

You win by **checkmate**: the enemy king is in check and no legal move can save it. A player may also resign. In timed games a player whose clock runs out loses, unless the opponent has no way left to give checkmate, in which case it is a draw.

The game is a draw when:

- the player to move is not in check but has no legal move (**stalemate**);
- neither side has enough material to checkmate, for example king against king, or king and bishop against king;
- both players agree to a draw;
- the same position appears for the third time with the same player to move, or fifty moves pass without a capture or a pawn move, and a player claims the draw.

In tournaments a win scores 1 point, a draw ½ and a loss 0.

## Common variants

- **Blitz and rapid**: the same rules played with a clock, typically 3 to 5 minutes per player for blitz and 10 to 60 minutes for rapid.
- **Chess960 (Fischer random)**: the back-rank pieces are shuffled into one of 960 starting positions and castling is adapted accordingly.
- **Touch-move**: under the FIDE Laws a piece you deliberately touch must be moved if it can; say "I adjust" before straightening one.

## Tips for first games

- Bring your knights and bishops out early and castle within the first ten moves.
- Fight for the four centre squares with pawns and pieces.
- Before every move, ask what your opponent's last move threatens.
- Learn the rough piece values (pawn 1, knight and bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9) to judge exchanges.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can the king capture pieces?** Yes, like any other piece, as long as the square it lands on is not attacked.

**Can a pawn move backwards or capture straight ahead?** No. Pawns only move forward and only capture diagonally.

**Is stalemate a win for the stronger side?** No. Stalemate is a draw, which is why a beginner with a big material lead should keep checking that the opponent still has a move.

**How many queens can I have?** As many as you can promote, so up to nine in theory. Use an upturned rook or a coin if your set has only one queen.
