## What you need

Six ordinary dice, a pencil and a score sheet. A cup to shake the dice helps. Farkle is a folk game with many names (Ten Thousand, Zilch, Greed, Hot Dice) and many sets of house rules; boxed editions sell their own scoring cards. Our printable farkle score sheet has the scoring chart on top and room for six players.

## Setup

Choose a scorekeeper. Each player rolls one die; the highest roll goes first and play passes to the left. Everyone starts at zero.

## How to play

1. On your turn, roll all six dice.
2. Set aside **at least one scoring die or combination** (see the chart). You keep a running total for the turn.
3. Now choose: **bank** the running total and pass the dice, or **roll the remaining dice** to add more.
4. If a roll shows nothing that scores, that is a **farkle**: you lose the whole running total for this turn and your turn ends.
5. If you manage to set aside all six dice, you have **hot dice**: roll all six again and keep adding to the same running total.

A combination only counts if it appears in a single roll. Two 5s set aside from one roll and a third 5 from the next roll are three single 5s (150), not three of a kind (500).

## Scoring and winning

These are the traditional values given on Wikipedia from the folk-game collection *Playing Grandma's Games*:

| Dice | Points |
|---|---|
| Each 1 | 100 |
| Each 5 | 50 |
| Three 1s | 1,000 |
| Three 2s / 3s / 4s / 5s / 6s | 200 / 300 / 400 / 500 / 600 |
| Four of a kind | 1,000 |
| Five of a kind | 2,000 |
| Six of a kind | 3,000 |
| Three pairs | 1,500 |
| Straight 1-2-3-4-5-6 | 2,500 |

When a player's banked total reaches **10,000** or more, every other player gets one last turn to beat it. The highest total then wins.

The boxed Farkle rules published by UltraBoardGames differ in places: three 1s score only 300, a straight scores 1,500, four of a kind with a pair scores 1,500 and two triplets 2,500. They also require a first bank of at least 500. Pick one table before you start and keep it for the whole game.

## Common variants

- **Opening threshold**: you cannot bank for the first time until your running total reaches 500 (or 1,000).
- **Doubling**: each extra matching die doubles three of a kind, so four 1s are 2,000 and five 1s 4,000.
- **Piggybacking**: you may start your turn by rolling the dice the previous player left, and take over their running total if you score.
- **Five-dice game**: the original form of the game used five dice; three pairs is impossible.
- **Three farkles**: three farkles in a row cost 500 points.
- **Exact 10,000**: you must land on exactly 10,000; overshoot and your turn's points go to the lowest player.

## Tips for first games

- With six dice, a farkle is rare, so keep rolling. With one or two dice left, the risk is high; bank unless you are far behind.
- You do not have to keep every scoring die. Setting aside one 1 and re-rolling five dice is often better than keeping a lone 5 as well.
- A popular simple plan: bank once your running total reaches 300 to 500, unless you have hot dice.
- In the final round, you know the target. Roll until you beat it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does "farkle" mean in the game?** A roll with no scoring dice. You lose the points from that turn, not your banked score.

**Do I have to set aside all scoring dice?** No. You must set aside at least one scoring die after each roll; the rest you may re-roll.

**Can I add dice from different rolls to make three of a kind?** No. Combinations must come from one roll.

**What are hot dice?** All six dice have scored in the turn. Roll all six again and keep adding to your total.

**How many points to win?** 10,000 is standard. Play to 5,000 for a quicker game.
