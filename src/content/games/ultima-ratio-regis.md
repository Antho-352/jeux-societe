## About Ultima Ratio Regis

Ultima Ratio Regis is a grand strategy wargame by Ignacio Torres Siles, with graphics by Jesús Peralta García, for 1 to 8 players. It covers the military and political history of Early Modern Europe, from the discovery of the Americas to the eve of the French Revolution. The whole span is 20 turns of 15 years, played as scenarios such as the Wars of Religion or the Thirty Years' War, or as a Grand Campaign from 1480 to 1790. Each player usually controls several major powers, and historical event cards change the rules as the centuries pass.

This is a big game: the publisher estimates two to four hours per turn, and building it means printing a 97 x 69 cm map and mounting about 800 counters. Version 3.0 completed the design and version 3.2 adds clarifications. The files are free from printandplay.games, which says it publishes its games under Creative Commons; a professional edition from Hexasim has been announced. It is for experienced wargamers and history fans who want a long campaign to build and play with a regular group, or alone.

## How to play in one minute

1. Choose a scenario for your player count. Players bid victory points in secret to pick their group of major powers, then deploy their forces on the map.
2. Each turn starts with new powers entering and a play deck built from that period's historical events plus generic events, dealt out in secret.
3. In each half turn, players take impulses: play cards for events or actions, build troops, fleets and fortresses, move armies strategically and tactically, fight battles and sieges, or raid trade by piracy.
4. Wars are declared with a casus belli and end in peace or truce; at the end of the turn, powers pay upkeep, collect income and score victory points.
5. The player with the most victory points when the scenario ends wins.
