## Which dot to dot for which age?

The number of dots is the best guide to difficulty.

- **Under 6 (10 to 17 dots):** a star, a heart, a fish, a ghost or a rocket. A simple outline for children learning to count.
- **Alphabet (A to Z):** a butterfly, a rabbit and a gingerbread man, drawn by joining the letters in order.
- **Ages 6 to 8 (18 to 40 dots):** a sailboat, a cat, a penguin, a dinosaur, a snowman or a turtle.
- **Ages 9 to 13 (53 to 59 dots):** a seahorse, an elephant and a kangaroo.
- **Teens and adults (81 to 172 dots):** from a camel and an octopus to a lion, a toucan and a steam locomotive. A long, calm puzzle that needs a sharp pencil.

## How they work

Each PDF has two pages. Page 1 is the puzzle, with the starting dot circled. Page 2 is the finished picture. The dots sit only on the corners and turns of the outline, so the shape stays a surprise until the last line.

Print page 1 alone if the child should not see the picture first, and use a coloured pencil so the line stands out from the dots.
